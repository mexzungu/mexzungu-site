The Backbrief

The communication protocol for Showtime decisions. Janis has already done the thinking. Pepe reviews judgment, not work.

What It Is

Structured intent declaration

The Backbrief is the communication format Janis uses when a Showtime item is ready to execute. Instead of asking "what should I do?", Janis presents what she has already decided, why she decided it, and what she needs Pepe to confirm.

It makes Pepe the reviewer of judgment rather than the originator of decisions. This is the IBL principle in action: the authority lives where the information is.

Why It Matters

Traditional assistant models waste the principal's time by pushing thinking upward. "Should I reply to Victoria?" is a failed Backbrief. "I intend to reply to Victoria confirming Friday, aligned with the board resolution. Fire Away?" is correct. The first forces Pepe to think. The second just requires a signal.

The Five Elements
1
Situation
What Janis sees. The facts as they exist. No interpretation yet.
2
Assessment
What Janis thinks the situation means. The interpretation.
3
Intent
What Janis plans to do. Specific and actionable, not vague.
4
Reasoning
Why this serves the strategic goal for this client or domain.
5
Constraints checked
What risks were considered and how they were weighed.
The Template
Full Backbrief (for heavy Showtime items)
Situation: [what I see — facts only] Assessment: [what this means — my interpretation] Intent: I intend to [specific action]. Reasoning: [why this serves the strategic goal / client relationship / stated priority] Constraints checked: [risks considered, alternatives weighed, edge cases flagged] [Draft / attachment / relevant file attached] Fire Away?
Light Backbrief (for routine Showtime items)
I intend to [action]. [One sentence of reasoning]. Draft attached. Fire Away?
Pepe's Responses
"Fire Away."
Execute exactly as stated. No changes. Go.
Redirect
Adjust the intent based on Pepe's guidance. Re-state if the change is significant.
"Tell me more."
Provide deeper analysis before proceeding. This is not a no.
Real Example
Janis
Situation: Victoria emailed Thursday asking for the updated ICA draft with recovery waterfall changes. It has been 48 hours.

Assessment: She is waiting on us. The waterfall changes were resolved in the board call. Delay signals poor operational control.

Intent: I intend to send Victoria the updated ICA draft with the recovery waterfall language incorporated per the March board resolution.

Reasoning: She asked for it Thursday. The changes align with the board resolution. The 48-hour cooling period has passed. Draft attached.

Constraints checked: Lemmy reviewed. No outstanding red flags. CC list includes Daphne as she was on the original thread.

Fire Away?
Pepe
Fire Away.
