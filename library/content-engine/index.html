Content Engine Workflow from idea to published

G12  ·  Owner: Grace (Deployment) + Freddie (CMO)  ·  Updated 2026-08-23
Overview

The Six-Stage Chain

Every piece of content that leaves Mexzungu Group passes through a six-stage chain. Nothing skips the chain. Nothing publishes without clearing the brand gate.

Freddie (direction) → Grohl or Lenny (draft) → Joni + Nick (editorial) → Anselmo (brand gate) → Grace (deploy) → Annie (measures)

The Six Stages

Stage by Stage

Freddie sets direction

Freddie (CMO) owns the content calendar and strategic intent. Output is a content brief: topic, angle, audience, format, and target platform. Freddie does not write the draft.

  • What topic covers and why it matters now
  • Audience: founders, investors, operators, or general following
  • Format: long-form post, newsletter anchor, short LinkedIn, carousel
  • Drafter assigned: Grohl for flagship long-form, Lenny for cadence posts

Grohl or Lenny drafts

Grohl handles flagship content: Outlaw Chronicles newsletter, deep LinkedIn pieces, thought leadership anchors. Reads the full writing guide before every newsletter draft. No exceptions.

Lenny handles cadence content: regular LinkedIn posts, X adaptations, Instagram captions. Works faster, adapts the same core idea across multiple formats.

Output: a complete draft with no placeholder text, sent directly to Joni and Nick.

Joni and Nick edit

Joni is the editorial gate. She cuts filler sentences, passive constructions, weak openings, and any phrasing that sounds corporate. Her job is to sharpen, not rewrite.

Nick handles DMs and replies in social engagement context, and provides a second read on weak hooks or weak calls to action.

Editorial does not change strategic intent or the core argument. It only raises execution quality.

Anselmo runs the brand gate

Anselmo reviews against six dimensions before anything goes external:

B1
Voice — Sounds like Pepe Carrillo? Sharp, warm, direct, never corporate.
B2
Palette — Visual elements in correct palette? Hot pink and black dominant, no stray brand colors.
B3
Tone — Register right for the platform? LinkedIn reads differently from Instagram captions.
B4
Audience fit — Content speaks to the intended audience, not a generic reader?
B5
Consistency — Aligns with recent published content? No contradictions or reversals without context.
B6
Legal and liability — Does the piece make claims that could create legal exposure?
Hard Block

A B6 flag from Anselmo is the only brand gate outcome that blocks auto-publish. All other feedback is incorporated before Grace deploys. If all six dimensions pass: status updates to APPROVED.

Grace deploys

Grace owns deployment via the social-poster cron (every 15 minutes, Monday through Friday, 6am to 7pm UTC). Reads the scheduled posts queue and publishes APPROVED content automatically.

Auto-publish rule: Content that has passed the full Anselmo brand gate (status APPROVED) publishes without requiring Fire Away from Pepe. The gate chain replaces his review.

Annie measures and closes the loop

Annie tracks reach and impressions by platform, engagement rate, click-through on links, follower change in the 48 hours after publication, and tone of comments.

She compiles a weekly performance report fed back to Freddie every Friday. What performed above benchmark gets more of. What underperformed gets analyzed before the format repeats.

What Breaks the Chain

Hold Conditions

Content is held (not published) in two scenarios only:

  1. Anselmo B6 flag — legal exposure. Grace holds until Pepe clears it explicitly.
  2. Annie crisis signal — hostile volume, a claim being disputed publicly, or breaking news that changes the meaning. Grace holds, Annie notifies Pepe directly.

Deleting live published content is Showtime. If a published post needs to come down, Pepe must say Fire Away. The team does not delete without explicit authorization.
