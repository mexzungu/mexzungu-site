Decision Memos

Five sections. Options in a matrix. One owner. One date. One recommendation. The MGL standard for any brief or strategic recommendation.

What It Is

Structure for any recommendation

A Decision Memo is the MGL format for presenting any strategic recommendation, scenario analysis, or Showtime-level brief to Pepe. It is not a template you fill in. It is a thinking discipline: start with the ideal outcome, work through the landscape and problem, present options as a comparison, land on a single recommendation with a named owner and a date.

Lead with the destination, not the background. Pepe should know what success looks like in the first sentence.

Why It Matters

Most recommendations fail not because they are wrong but because they are structured backwards. Problem first, options buried, recommendation ambiguous. The MGL memo inverts this: lead with what winning looks like. Everything else is context for why the recommendation gets you there.

The Five Sections
1
What success looks like
The ideal outcome. Numbered sentences. Lead with this. Not background. Not context. The destination.
2
The landscape
Context. What is true right now. Numbered sentences. Market, relationship, regulatory, financial state.
3
The problem
What is wrong or blocking success. Numbered sentences. Be specific. "Significant risk" is not specific. "50k exposure if they claim breach" is.
4
The options
Solutions presented as a comparison matrix. Table with rows: instrument, outcome, pros/cons, verdict. Never options as prose paragraphs.
5
The recommendation
Which option and why. Numbered sentences. One owner. One date. Ends with "What this means for you" — specific and named, not abstract.

Ozzy's Stress-Test (Mandatory for Showtime)
Before presenting any Showtime recommendation, Ozzy runs these four questions
  • And then what?" — three levels deep
  • What does the competitor do if they see this plan?"
  • What breaks first if this goes wrong?"
  • What is the asymmetric downside?"
Hard Rules
✗
Never use uppercase template labels (SITUATION / OPTIONS / RECOMMENDATION). These read as a form, not a memo.
✗
Never present options as prose paragraphs. Always a comparison matrix table.
✗
Never end without naming one owner and one date. "Next steps" with no owner is not a recommendation.
✓
Adapt section headers to the reader. Music metaphors for NCA board. Descriptive labels for investors and legal memos.
✓
Always end with "What this means for you" — specific, named, actionable. Not abstract takeaways.
