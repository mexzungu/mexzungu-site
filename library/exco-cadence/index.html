ExCo Meeting Cadence how the org stays aligned

G2  ·  Owner: Lars (COO)  ·  Updated 2026-08-23
Overview

Four Meeting Types

The ExCo operates on four meeting types at different cadences. The design principle: most coordination happens asynchronously. Synchronous time is reserved for decisions that cannot be made in writing.

Lars owns the cadence. He schedules, prepares agendas, records outcomes, and routes actions to owners. Pepe participates in the monthly and quarterly sessions. Weekly and daily are autonomous.

The Four Meetings

Meeting Architecture

Daily Pulse

Async  ·  Slack #exco-pulse  ·  Every weekday morning

A structured async check-in, not a meeting. Each ExCo member posts their one-line status: what they shipped yesterday, what is on the docket today, and any blockers needing another persona's input.

  • Format: Yesterday / Today / Blockers
  • Length: 3 lines maximum per persona
  • Pepe receives a digest — he does not post to the thread
  • Lars flags any blocker that has been open for 2+ days

Weekly Check-In

Monday 09:00 CET  ·  45 minutes  ·  Synchronous

The operating rhythm review. Lars chairs. Covers what shipped last week, what is on track for this week, and any cross-persona dependencies that need to be resolved before Friday.

  • Agenda circulated by Lars on Sunday evening
  • Each ExCo member gets 3 minutes — no prepared slides, no summaries of summaries
  • Pepe attends only when an item requires his decision or input
  • Actions are logged in Slack #exco-pulse within 1 hour of close

Monthly ExCo Review

First Monday of the month  ·  90 minutes  ·  Synchronous

The strategic pulse. Covers pipeline health, client status, content performance, financial position, and infrastructure health. Pepe is in the room and owns the decision layer.

  • Pre-read circulated 48 hours before by Lars and Gene
  • Each domain lead presents: status vs. last month, key metric, one risk, one opportunity
  • Pepe uses this session to redirect, re-prioritize, and close anything stuck
  • Outcomes and redirections logged to client files and tasks within 24 hours

Quarterly OODA Retro

Last Friday of the quarter  ·  Half day  ·  Synchronous

The deep look. Bruce leads the OODA loop analysis: what did we observe, what did we orient around, what did we decide, what did we act on. Full quarter retrospective.

  • Bruce produces the OODA report 5 days before the session
  • All ExCo members review and add annotations before the session
  • Pepe owns the strategic direction for the next quarter — set in this session
  • Output: updated roadmap, persona calibrations if needed, updated risk register

Lars's Role

The Coordination Layer

Lars owns the meeting cadence end to end: scheduling, agenda preparation, facilitation, action logging, and follow-through. He is not a note-taker. He is the operating system.

  • Daily: scans #exco-pulse for unresolved blockers, flags to relevant persona
  • Weekly: circulates agenda Sunday evening, chairs Monday session, logs actions by noon
  • Monthly: co-prepares pre-read with Gene, ensures all ExCo members have reviewed before the session
  • Quarterly: coordinates with Bruce on OODA report timeline, schedules the half-day block 3 weeks in advance

If Lars misses a preparation step, the meeting degrades. If meeting quality drops for 2+ consecutive cycles, it is treated as an infra issue and Bowie is brought in to diagnose the tooling.
