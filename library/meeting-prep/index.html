Meeting Prep Guide the pre-call research protocol

Client Operations 5.8  ·  Owner: Sade (Client Success)  ·  Updated 2026-08-23
The Principle

Every Call Should Feel Briefed

Every call Pepe walks into should feel like a call with someone who already knows the situation. Not because Pepe memorizes notes, but because Janis pulls the relevant context 30 minutes before the call starts and surfaces it in a format that takes two minutes to read.

The meeting-prep cron runs every 30 minutes during waking hours. It scans the calendar for calls starting within the next 30 to 60 minutes and fires a prep brief to Pepe on Telegram.

TriggerCondition
Default triggerAny calendar event with a video link (Meet, Zoom, Teams) or marked as call, discovery, client meeting, or intro
Manual triggerPepe messages "prep me for [name/company]" at any time
No prep neededInternal async items, reminders, and blocked time
Research Sequence

What Janis Pulls

  1. Email history (last 30 days) — All four inboxes searched for the counterparty's name and email domain. Last substantive exchange summarized in two sentences: what was asked, what was said, what is open.
  2. Contact profile (Google Contacts) — Full contact record pulled: name, company, title, phone, notes. If a contact file exists in brain/contacts/, read it for relationship context and standing notes.
  3. Client overview — If active client or prospect, their overview.md is read. Extract: current status, open items, billing situation, any flags.
  4. Open tasks and tickets — Lars's ticket system checked for any open items tagged to this client. Overdue items flagged prominently.
  5. Recent WhatsApp or Telegram thread — Personal and business WhatsApp searched. If active conversation, the last few messages take priority over email.
Brief Format

The Four-Section Structure

Every prep brief follows the same four-section structure. No exceptions. The entire brief fits in a single Telegram message.

Headline

One sentence: who the call is with, what it is about, when it starts.
"Call with Victoria Peill (Duara) in 30 min. Topic: Q3 billing and Stella SAFE assignment."

Key Context

Three to five bullet points. Relationship history, last exchange summary, outstanding commitments from either side.

Open Items

Flat list of anything open, overdue, or promised but not yet delivered. If nothing open: "No open items."

Suggested Agenda

Two to three talking points based on open items and context. Not a script, a frame. Pepe uses these or ignores them.

Delivery: Telegram, plain text, no bold markdown, no hash headers. Readable on a phone screen. If the brief does not fit in one Telegram message, it is too long.

New Contacts

Extended Research Protocol

When Pepe has a call with someone not yet in Google Contacts, Janis runs extended research before the brief:

  • LinkedIn: current role, company, background, recent public posts (Playwright scraping where needed)
  • Company search: company name, size, location, funding history if applicable, recent news (Google, Crunchbase, LinkedIn company page)
  • Mutual connections: Google Contacts searched for anyone at the same company or shared network. Warm context is leverage.
  • Source of intro: who made the introduction, when, and what was said. Sets the relational frame.

Extended brief is allowed to be slightly longer than the standard brief, but must still fit in two Telegram messages maximum.

Quality Standard

Good Brief vs. Bad Brief

Good brief

  • Takes 90 seconds to read
  • Contains one or two things Pepe could not have remembered without it
  • Has at least one open item surfaced
  • Suggested agenda reflects actual relationship context
  • Accurate: dates, names, amounts verified against source files

Bad brief

  • Restates what Pepe already knows from the calendar invite
  • Three paragraphs of background on a 2-year client
  • No open items listed despite overdue invoices
  • Suggested agenda does not match the actual call context
  • Late, or missing entirely
Edge Cases

What to Do When

ScenarioResponse
Call Pepe did not bookStill trigger prep. Source of the calendar event does not matter.
Confidential or sensitive callPrep brief still sent. Be more careful about what is included, not whether to send.
Call reschedules within the 30-minute windowUpdate and resend the brief.
Call cancelsNo action needed.
Back-to-back meetingsSend the brief as early as possible with a note: "Early prep — you have back-to-back at [time]."
Limited context availableSay so clearly in the brief. Never fabricate. "Limited context available — new contact, no prior email history."
