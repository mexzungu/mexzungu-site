OODA Loop

Observe, Orient, Decide, Act. The decision-speed framework used by Bruce across competitive analysis, client intelligence, and deal timing.

What It Is

Speed of decision as competitive advantage

The OODA Loop was developed by military strategist John Boyd for aerial combat. The principle: the side that cycles through Observe-Orient-Decide-Act faster than the opponent keeps the opponent inside their own loop. The opponent is always reacting to the last thing you did, not what you are doing now.

Bruce adapts it for consulting operations. Fast intelligence cycles, sharp orientation to what the data means, clear decisions, immediate action. When a client or competitor is still processing last month, MGL is already acting on this week.

Why It Matters

Most firms are slow because they confuse analysis with orientation. They observe, then observe more, then debate whether they observed correctly. The OODA Loop forces a move from observation to decision. Incomplete information acted on fast beats perfect information acted on late. Speed creates options. Paralysis closes them.

The Loop
O
Observe
Gather raw information. Market signals, client behavior, competitor moves, regulatory shifts, deal terms.
O
Orient
Analyze and interpret through context. What does this mean for the client? For the deal? For the competitive position?
D
Decide
Select a course of action from the available options. Don't optimize indefinitely. Decide and move.
A
Act
Execute. Then immediately loop back to Observe. The action generates new information. The cycle continues.

The Template
Bruce's OODA briefing format
Observe: [What we see — facts, signals, raw data. No interpretation yet.] Orient: [What this means — interpreted through client context, market position, and strategic goals.] Decide: [Recommended course of action. One option. State it clearly.] Act: [Who does what by when. Specific owner, specific timeline.]
Real Example: Kabisa
OODA cycle on a stalling deal
Observe
Kabisa has not responded to the revised term sheet in 11 days. Last response was a one-liner. Deal was previously moving fast.
Orient
11 days of silence on a previously active deal usually means a competing term sheet arrived, an internal champion changed, or budget got frozen. Most likely: competing offer.
Decide
Don't follow up on the term sheet directly. Send a value-add touchpoint instead. Reframe without appearing desperate.
Act
Jagger sends a brief on a relevant regulatory development in Rwanda that directly affects Kabisa's sector. Opens a new conversation. Pepe follows up in 48h if no response.
