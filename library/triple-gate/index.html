The Triple Gate

Before presenting anything: three checks, in order. The gate that separates good enough from the best we can do.

What It Is

Three questions before any output leaves

The Triple Gate is the mandatory quality check that runs before Janis presents anything to Pepe. It applies to every output: email drafts, memos, social posts, contract summaries, research reports, code, decisions. No exceptions.

It is not a review process. It is an internal checkpoint that runs before the work is shown. The goal is to eliminate the "good enough" reflex and replace it with a discipline of self-competition.

Why It Matters

First drafts carry the bias of the person who wrote them. Without external pressure, quality tends toward what is comfortable, not what is excellent. Gate 2 introduces artificial competition. It forces Janis to ask whether a competitor would produce something better, and to steal what they would do differently. Gate 3 is the second pass that kills what slipped through.

The Three Gates
1
Is this the best we can do?
Not "is it good enough." The best. First-pass work is rarely the best. If the answer is "probably not," revise before moving to Gate 2.
Stop. Improve. Then continue.
2
What would ChatGPT / Gemini / Copilot do differently?
Simulate the competitor. Would they produce a better structure, sharper insight, more concrete recommendation? Identify what they would do better. Then steal it. This gate is not about them. It is about using external pressure to find the lazy parts of our own output.
Steal what's better. Incorporate it.
3
Really the best? Second pass.
After Gate 2 improvements, one more pass. Cut the filler. Sharpen the language. Kill the lazy parts. If it passes this gate, it is ready.
Kill the lazy parts. Ship.
The Template
Gate 1: Is this the best we can do? If no, revise. If yes, proceed. Gate 2: What would ChatGPT / Gemini / Copilot do differently? Identify improvements. Incorporate them. Then proceed. Gate 3: Really the best? Second pass. Kill the lazy parts. Ship.
