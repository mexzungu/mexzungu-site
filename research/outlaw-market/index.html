2. Content Gaps Nobody Is Filling

Eight topic areas where demand is documented in founder forums, legal communities, and Twitter threads, but where no newsletter provides authoritative, structured coverage:

1. Founder divorce and cap table

Total taboo in startup media. The mechanics of co-founder buyouts, forced vesting acceleration, and dead equity cleanup are searched constantly but never written about with real math by someone who has executed them.

2. Reverse acqui-hire trap

The Windsurf $2.4B example. Acqui-hires structured so the acquiree's team gets employment contracts while shareholders get nothing. Most founders don't understand the structure until it's too late.

3. Delaware flip mechanics for African and LatAm founders

The cost structure, timing, tax implications, and when NOT to flip. Global founders are doing this wrong and paying for it at Series A. Zero authoritative newsletter coverage.

4. The "after it goes wrong" playbook

Dead equity, employee buybacks, being fired by your board, zombie companies. The failure mechanics nobody wants to write about because they require lived experience to do correctly.

5. Cross-border ESOP tax traps

Country-by-country tax treatment of stock options. Kenya, Nigeria, Rwanda, Mexico, Spain, UK all have different treatment. No newsletter maps this for global founding teams.

6. Side letters and secret investor terms

Most founders don't know side letters exist until they're in due diligence and one surfaces. The standard clauses, what's negotiable, and what locks you in forever.

7. AI-generated IP ownership

If your product is built on Claude or GPT, who owns what? The jurisdiction gaps, investor DD risks, and how to structure IP assignments for AI-native companies.

8. 83(b) and QSBS strategy post-2025 law changes

Updated rules, the filing window mechanics, and the combination strategies for US-based founders and founders relocating to the US for their raise.

5. The Open Lane

The research conclusion is not that the newsletter market is uncrowded. It is specifically crowded at the product/growth/trends layer and specifically empty at the structural/legal/cross-border layer.

Founders in Africa and LatAm raising from US investors are navigating Delaware law, Kenyan or Nigerian regulations, and SAFE mechanics simultaneously. No newsletter speaks to this reader. The Outlaw Chronicles is the first one that does it with the voice of someone who has executed these structures in the field, not just read about them.

The analogy: Lenny's built authority in product because Patrick Campbell built authority in pricing and nobody else was doing it with that depth and that voice. The Outlaw Chronicles has the same opportunity in legal structure for global founders. The lane is not just open. It is actively underserved.
