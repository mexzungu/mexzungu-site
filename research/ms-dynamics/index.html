Key Findings

  • Submission activates a cortisol-endocannabinoid-oxytocin cascade with no pharmacological equivalent. The neurological state is real, distinct, and requires practice to achieve.
  • Wismeijer & van Assen (2013, n=936): BDSM practitioners score lower on neuroticism, higher on wellbeing, and less rejection-sensitive than controls. Dominants show the most favorable profile.
  • Baumeister (1988): the strongest people are the most drawn to submission, not because they have less self, but because they carry more self and need the escape.
  • D/s is a role. M/s is an identity. The jump requires will-surrender, not just behavioral compliance, and a holding environment built over time.
  • "Dominated physically while maintaining mental control" is Benjamin's thirdness preserved under objectification. It is not resistance; it is the appropriate starting point for a dynamic whose container has not yet earned full surrender.
  • The Switch's choice to submit to one specific person while Dominating others is not contradiction, it is identity completeness. Bennett (2025) documents this as a recognized, psychologically distinct subtype.
  • The anxiously-attached partner's regulatory benefit from M/s structure is real and significant, but the Dominant's consistency is load-bearing. The system amplifies both good input and bad input.

1. Neuroscience and Psychophysiology

The most comprehensive synthesis is Wuyts and Morrens (2021), The Biology of BDSM: A Systematic Review, Journal of Sexual Medicine. What follows is the empirical picture, system by system.

The Neurochemical Cascade

Sagarin et al. found cortisol rose significantly in submissives during BDSM interactions, but not in dominants. The body activates its threat system; the mind codes the activation as rewarding. This cortisol rise co-occurred with elevated endocannabinoid levels. The two systems firing simultaneously is the neurological signature of controlled arousal: real physiological stress, real subjective pleasure. Not performance. A distinct biological state.

Endocannabinoids (anandamide and 2-AG, the body's endogenous THC equivalents) increased significantly in submissives. For dominants, endocannabinoid increases only appeared in those focused on relational authority, not physical sensation alone. The neurochemical pathway differs depending on whether the practice is physically sensational or relationally authoritative. Power itself activates a distinct cascade in the Dominant.

Neurochemical Profile: Submissive vs Dominant
Relative change in key neurochemicals during consensual power exchange. Source: Wuyts & Morrens (2021), Sagarin et al.

Endorphins (endogenous opioids, same receptors as morphine) are released during intense physical experiences. Pain experienced as chosen and held in a safe container activates endorphin release differently from pain experienced as threatening. The context of consent is not merely psychological, it is doing real biological work. Larva and Rantala (2024, Archives of Sexual Behavior) confirmed sexual arousal modulates pain thresholds significantly, with vaginal stimulation doubling tolerance in experimental conditions.

Oxytocin, released during deep power-exchange interactions, promotes pair-bonding, suppresses inflammatory pain through direct receptor action, and makes vulnerability feel safe. This is why aftercare produces disproportionate intimacy relative to its surface content. The combination of cortisol stress, endorphin euphoria, and oxytocin bonding is a cocktail with no pharmaceutical equivalent.

Subspace: Transient Hypofrontality

The 2016 Northern Illinois University study (Sagarin, Lee et al.) was the first to empirically examine BDSM-induced altered states. Using the Stroop test, submissives showed what Arne Dietrich (2003, Consciousness and Cognition) calls transient hypofrontality: temporary down-regulation of prefrontal cortex activity. The PFC governs executive function, self-monitoring, anxiety, decision-making. When it quiets, the subjective experience is the floaty, ego-minimized quality practitioners call subspace. Not dissociation. A genuine neurologically-distinct state.

Subspace vs Topspace: Altered State Profiles
Two distinct neurological states operating simultaneously in the same interaction. Source: Sagarin et al. (2016), Pitagora (2017), Dietrich (2003).

Critical asymmetry: submissives showed decreased cognitive flexibility (hypofrontality). Dominants maintained or improved cognitive performance, consistent with Csikszentmihalyi's flow model: complete absorption, heightened focus. Kamping et al. using fMRI confirmed that identical painful stimuli were reported as substantially reduced in masochistic versus neutral contexts, the psychological framing physically alters how pain is processed in the parietal operculum, anterior insula, and sensorimotor cortex.

Flow State

Pitagora (2017, Journal of Positive Sexuality) was the first to formally apply Csikszentmihalyi's flow framework to BDSM. Practitioners consistently report: world drops away, complete focus, loss of time sense, post-scene mood improvement. Topping maps precisely onto classical flow (clear goals, challenge-skill balance, absorbed action). Bottoming maps onto transient hypofrontality plus selected flow facets: loss of self-consciousness, time distortion, intrinsic reward. Both states require practice and skill to achieve reliably.

2. Depth Psychology

Baumeister: Masochism as Escape from Self

Roy Baumeister's foundational 1988 paper "Masochism as Escape from Self" (Journal of Sex Research, 25:1) offered the first non-pathological account of submission. His argument: masochism functions as a mechanism for temporarily escaping the burden of high-level self-awareness.

In contemporary life, the self is symbolically mediated, temporally extended, and morally accountable. Managing reputation across time, maintaining a coherent narrative identity, monitoring performance against internal standards, this is cognitively expensive and creates chronic self-monitoring pressure. Masochism provides escape by forcing attention entirely into the present body, preventing symbolic abstract self-referential processing.

The Paradox

The more elaborated and burdensome the self-concept, the stronger the pull toward this escape. Highly individuated, high-achieving people do not submit because they have less self, they submit because they have more self and therefore more to escape from. Baumeister extended this in "Escaping the Self" (1991) to cover meditation, alcohol, and spiritual practice. The impulse is universal. The BDSM version is structured, reversible, and done with a witness.

Jessica Benjamin: Mutual Recognition and Thirdness

Jessica Benjamin (The Bonds of Love, 1988; Beyond Doer and Done To, 2004) distinguishes pathological dominance from consensual power exchange through her concept of thirdness: the ongoing awareness of the shared intersubjective field, in which both partners remain full subjects even within a highly asymmetric relational role. A healthy M/s dynamic maintains thirdness because both parties can recognize the other's full personhood.

Physical objectification within a thirdness-preserving frame is not pathological but psychologically sophisticated. The partner who wants to be "dominated physically while maintaining mental control" is, in Benjamin's framework, insisting on preserving thirdness during physical objectification, submitting the body while the mind remains a co-subject. This is more sophisticated than complete surrender, not less.

Winnicott: The Holding Environment

Winnicott's "holding environment" enables what he called regression in the service of the ego: return to more vulnerable states within a structurally secure frame. A skilled Dominant's consistent, reliable authority functions as Winnicott's holding environment. The protocols, negotiation, and aftercare are the operational elements of that container. The JVA's entire architecture is holding environment construction. The container is built before being named.

"The slave cannot surrender more than the container has proven it can hold."

Practitioner principle, consistent with Winnicott's developmental framework

Frankfurt: The Surrender Paradox

Harry Frankfurt's "Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person" (1971) resolves the apparent paradox of choosing to surrender choice. Frankfurt distinguishes first-order desires (what I want in this moment) from second-order volitions (what I want my wants to be). The slave chooses at the second-order level to be someone who obeys. This meta-level choice is the location of freedom. First-order obedience is consistent with second-order autonomy. The self that sets the rules of the game is not the same as the self that plays within them.

4. The Philosophy of Consensual Slavery

Hegel: Useful and Wrong

Hegel's Lordship-Bondsman dialectic (Phenomenology of Spirit, 1807) argues the Master's recognition by the Slave is valueless because the Slave's unfreedom depletes it. In consensual M/s, this is wrong: the slave's recognition retains its full value precisely because the slave remains a full agent choosing to offer it. The recognition is not coerced. Their ongoing consent is the ongoing gift of recognition to someone they judge worthy of it. Hegel's dialectic requires a slave with no exit. M/s requires a slave who chooses not to use it.

Foucault: Power as Relational

Foucault's power framework (Discipline and Punish, 1975; The Subject and Power, 1982) argues that power is not a possession but a relation, it circulates, is exercised rather than held, and is always potentially reversible. In a 1982 interview, Foucault described S/M as "a strategic game of freedom" rather than an abuse of power: power relationships that are agonistic (contested, alive) within a context of mutual consent.

Parchev and Langdridge (2018, Sexualities) extended this: M/s can represent a form of resistance to normative sexuality through strategic subversion, taking the forms of power and deploying them reflexively, consciously, with explicit consent as their condition of possibility.

6. Switch Identity: The Academic Picture

Bennett (2025), "Switch it up: A qualitative analysis of BDSM switches" (Sexualities, Sage) is the most current empirical work. Central finding: being a Switch is a distinct identity category, not behavioral flexibility. Switches report their identity could only be fully expressed by enacting both dominant and submissive roles. A Switch who can only submit is experiencing identity incompleteness.

Switch Identity Architecture
The Switch does not alternate roles within one relationship, they hold two complete identity positions across different relationships. Source: Bennett (2025), Rambukkana (2017).

Rambukkana (2017, Journal of Homosexuality) using mixed methods found Switches demonstrate notably high psychological flexibility, openness to experience, and role mobility. They are not confused about who they are. They have a broader identity structure that encompasses both positions, psychologically more sophisticated than fixed-role identity, not less.

The Specific Subtype: Dominant Elsewhere, Slave Here

Bennett documents this as a recognized subtype. Switches often organize their dual identity around relationship-specific factors rather than domain-specific ones. The question "why this person specifically" is an identity question: he activates the submissive pole of a dual identity that genuinely requires both positions for full expression. Her submission here is not something that happens despite her dominant identity elsewhere. It is enabled by it.

The Consistency Principle

The practitioner and academic consensus is uniform: constantly switching roles within the same M/s dynamic breaks down the authority structure. Within this specific relationship, the axis is fixed: he is Master, she is slave. Her dominant expression in other relationships is a separate identity register, not a contradiction. The M/s structure between them is not one that gets reversed.

7. Sociology and Community

Parchev (2023/2025): TPE and the Inclusion/Exclusion Problem

Ofer Parchev, "BDSM and total power exchange: Between inclusion and exclusion" (Sexualities, Sage, 2023) makes a sophisticated argument: the social legitimization of BDSM through the 2000s-2020s required presenting BDSM as consent-based, equal, and bounded. This secured mainstream tolerance but simultaneously excluded TPE and M/s, precisely because M/s is structurally incompatible with the SSC (Safe, Sane, Consensual) consent architecture that normalization deployed.

In M/s, power division is unequal by design. The slave's consent is given once and periodically renewed, not moment-by-moment. The dynamic is continuous, not scene-bounded. These features violate SSC. TPE thus faces exclusion from both vanilla culture and the normalized BDSM community. The practical consequence: M/s is practiced today with less community scaffolding than the Old Guard Leather tradition provided in the 1960s-80s.

Old Guard Leather: The Historical Container

The Old Guard Leather community (post-WWII gay male motorcycle clubs, 1940s-50s) developed the most formalized M/s tradition in Western BDSM history. Key features: collars earned over months or years (never given casually), strict protocols governing social interaction between masters and slaves in community settings, skill mastery as prerequisite for Master status, mentorship chains. The community provided all functions Bowlby attributed to secure attachment networks: identity, belonging, mentorship, and shared meaning framework.

Population Research

Richters et al. (2008, Australian Study, n=19,370): BDSM practitioners were not more likely to have been coerced into sexual activity and showed no elevated psychological distress compared to non-practitioners. This study effectively dismantled the clinical assumption that BDSM practice is a pathology marker. Wismeijer and van Assen (2013): practitioners showed more favorable psychological profiles across all key dimensions.

8. Trauma, Healing, and Risk

Gewirtz-Meydan et al. (2024, Journal of Sex and Marital Therapy, 50:5) provide the most current review of the trauma-BDSM intersection. Core finding: BDSM among trauma survivors is heterogeneous. For some, repetition compulsion, regaining control by reenacting with different outcomes. For some, dissociation makes the practice a trigger rather than resolution. For some, genuine therapeutic reclamation of bodily agency within a safe container.

Protective factors (Dunkley and Brotto, 2018): strong consent architecture; explicit negotiation skills; reliable aftercare; practitioner self-knowledge; ability to distinguish trauma replay from genuine preference.

Monitoring Flag

The self-acknowledged pattern of "using dating as a coping mechanism when too dependent" is risk-aware self-disclosure from someone who has done the psychological work to name their pattern. It is healthy that it was named. The monitoring responsibility is distributed: she watches for this in herself; the Dominant watches for the external markers. The test is whether autonomy, outside functioning, and self-regard grow or shrink over time.

9. Spirituality and Transcendence

Carlström (2021): Empirical Evidence

Charlotta Carlström (Sexualities, Sage, 2021) conducted the first peer-reviewed study specifically examining spiritual experiences in BDSM, with direct comparisons to Christian mysticism. Four components of BDSM spiritual experience identified: an embodied sense of an energetic force; a sense of spiritual presence; a self-surrendered and transcended state of consciousness; a deeply personal and lasting transformation. The structural parallels to mystical literature are not metaphorical, the altered states are neurologically comparable.

The Psilocybin Connection

Psilocybin at therapeutic doses produces ego dissolution through decreased activity in the default mode network (Carhart-Harris et al., 2014, PNAS), the brain's self-referential processing center. This is the same system Dietrich's transient hypofrontality describes as reduced in deep subspace. Different pharmacological path, same neurological destination.

Implication

A person with experience of psilocybin-held ego dissolution has a neurological reference point for what full surrender feels like. This lowers the psychological barrier to deeper submission. It also provides a comparative frame: is this M/s container as safely held as the ceremonial container? If not, the deeper surrender will not emerge. The quality of the holding environment determines how far the surrender can go. The JVA is not preliminary to the M/s dynamic, it is the M/s dynamic's foundation.

The Virgin/Whore Resolution

The "reconciling the virgin and the whore" framing, and its parallel across Jungian (Madonna/femme fatale split of the anima), tantric (sacred erotic feminine), and clinical (sexuality split from dignity) traditions, describes the same psychological project. BDSM, specifically submission to a Dominant who simultaneously holds the person in full respect while subjecting them to physical intensity, can resolve this split by demonstrating both can be true simultaneously. She can be fully surrendered and fully respected. This is structurally what the Committee/Candidate framework already delivered: the ceremony and the respect were the holding environment for the intensity. The M/s dynamic is the next iteration of the same resolution.

10. M/s in the ENM Architecture

The Structural Question

In M/s within ENM, the Master's authority may formally extend to the slave's other relationships. The slave's outside connections exist within the M/s framework, not alongside it as equivalents. This requires the Dominant to manage clearly with outside partners who need to understand the dynamic is a formal authority structure.

The critical open question: does submission include submission of outside relationship choices? This is the most significant scope decision in the early M/s architecture. Extending authority over outside connections prematurely, before the primary bond is deep and stable, is a recognized risk in the M/s community. It should follow consolidation of the primary bond, not precede it.

ENM Strain on the Primary Bond

For the anxiously-attached partner (AX:55), authority signals from the Dominant are under more strain in ENM contexts because time and attention are genuinely divided. Every occasion of absence registers as data about the attachment figure's availability. The Dominant's consistency under conditions of divided attention, not exclusive availability, is what the M/s structure gets tested on. Balzarini et al. (2018-2022 series) consistently find ENM practitioners demonstrate more sophisticated jealousy processing: interpreting it as information rather than catastrophe. This is the operational standard for the M/s ENM dynamic: jealousy as data, not structural threat.

11. ENM Within M/s: Implementation, Mechanics, Risks, and Gaps

Section 10 established the structural questions. This section addresses how M/s and ENM are combined in practice: the authority architecture, the protocols that make it functional, the failure modes, and the places where research has not yet caught up with lived practice.

The Directional Question: Which Frame Contains the Other

The foundational design decision precedes all protocols. Is this M/s within ENM, or ENM within M/s? The configuration determines which framework governs when the two sets of rules conflict.

ConfigurationWhat It MeansGoverning Principle
M/s Contains ENM The M/s authority structure is superordinate. Outside connections exist by permission of, and remain subordinate to, the Master's authority. The slave does not have other relationships in the polyamorous sense. The Master has extended access privileges to the slave. Authority determines hierarchy. ENM is a modality the Master sanctions, not a parallel structure. Aligns with TPE (Parchev, 2023).
ENM Contains M/s The M/s bond is one node in a broader network where all connections retain theoretical autonomy. The slave retains polyamorous agency. The M/s dynamic operates within that network but does not govern it. Mutual consent determines hierarchy. The M/s connection is intensely structured internally but does not determine the architecture of other connections.

Parchev (2023) identifies a structural tension in TPE M/s within ENM: if authority is total, the outside connection is never the slave's relationship in the polyamorous sense. It is a connection the slave conducts with the Master's license. Whether this contradiction can be sustained long-term is an open empirical question. In practice, most M/s dyads with active ENM components operate on a hybrid. Formal authority in the M/s frame combined with practical operational latitude for the slave's outside connections.

Extending scope before the primary bond has consolidated creates competing loyalties the structure cannot yet absorb (Bisbey, 2022). The authority relationship needs depth before it can hold weight under outside-connection stress.

Implementation: The Protocols That Make It Functional

The Inform Immediately Protocol

In M/s ENM, delayed disclosure of new connections is not a communication failure. It is an authority failure. When the slave develops or advances a connection without immediate notification, the Master is retroactively placed in the position of adjudicating a situation that has already developed its own momentum. The window for clean authority-consistent decision-making closes the longer disclosure is delayed.

Bisbey (2022) is direct on this: "Being forthcoming, complete and honest with information. Whenever information needs to be shared, do so immediately even before inquiring." Veaux and Rickert (2014) establish the foundational principle: the right to privacy is not a right to secrecy, and facts that materially affect the relational network require disclosure. In M/s, this is not merely an ethical norm between equals. It is a structural requirement of the authority relationship.

Three cascading effects follow delayed disclosure:

  • Authority erosion. The slave has, in effect, made a unilateral decision. Even if the decision would have been sanctioned, the act of non-disclosure is an act of autonomous operation outside the authority structure.
  • Trust rupture disproportionate to the act. Research on deception in relationships (Drigotas et al., 1999) establishes that violation of relational expectations generates trust damage independent of the underlying behavior. In M/s, where the slave's transparency is constitutive of the dynamic, this damage strikes the structural foundation.
  • NRE contamination of the disclosure. When disclosure finally occurs inside New Relationship Energy, the slave presents the connection from within an altered emotional state that compromises their capacity to foreground the Master's interests.

Labriola (2013) identifies the discovery-versus-disclosure distinction as load-bearing: discovery implies the partner was not prioritized; disclosure implies they were. In M/s, this maps directly onto whether the authority relationship was honored.

Permission vs. Notification

  • Permission model. The slave requests authorization before initiating or advancing any outside connection. The Master responds before any action occurs. This is the classical M/s model. It preserves authority but requires the Master to be accessible for timely decisions.
  • Notification model. The slave informs contemporaneously or after the fact. The Master may respond with direction but the slave acts on their own initiative. This is a delegated-latitude structure: standing permission implicitly granted, subject to real-time reporting.

Balzarini et al. (2017) found that veto power in hierarchical polyamory was rarely exercised but was psychologically significant as a marker of primary-partner status. In M/s, the permission model serves an analogous structural function: it marks the authority relationship as superordinate even when, in practice, the Master grants most requests.

Time and Presence as Authority Functions

In M/s, time and presence are not relational resources to be negotiated. They are domains of authority. The Master's claim on the slave's time is constitutive of the dynamic. Simula (2019) documents that TPE relationships involve "explicit negotiation of the terms of dominance" extending to scheduling, presence, and attention allocation. Balzarini et al. (2017) found that primary partners in hierarchical polyamory receive more time, and that this differential correlated with higher primary-relationship quality. The M/s structure formalizes what polyamorous couples negotiate situationally: outside connections receive time the Master has sanctioned, not time the slave has allocated autonomously.

Mechanics: Jealousy, Compersion, and the Authority Frame

In standard CNM, jealousy signals a potential threat and must be processed collaboratively between equals. In M/s, the question of whether an outside connection is a threat is structurally resolved by the authority relationship: if the Master sanctioned it, it is within the structure's containment. This does not eliminate jealousy. Balzarini (2021) establishes that prior experience with a partner's extradyadic involvement and cognitive empathy do more work than structure alone. But it provides a framing device. Jealousy in M/s can be processed as information about unmet needs, not as evidence of structural rupture.

Balzarini et al. (2021) documented that compersion and jealousy are not opposing constructs. They co-occur. Prior experience with a partner's outside involvement predicted more compersion and less jealousy over time. In M/s, compersion may carry an additional dimension not studied empirically: the Master's satisfaction in the slave's pleasure through another may be inseparable from the authority dynamic itself. The connection occurs with the Master's sanction. The granting of that sanction is an expression of authority, not a challenge to it. This functional difference between M/s compersion and polyamorous-partner compersion has not been studied. It is a gap.

When jealousy arises in the Master, the productive processing question is not "how do I stop the outside connection" but "what is this telling me about what I need from the authority relationship that is not currently present." Labriola (2013) identifies jealousy as containing valid purpose, signaling unmet needs. Authority exercised from emotional reactivity rather than considered judgment erodes the slave's trust in the structure as principled.

NRE Management: Maintaining Authority Through New Relationship Energy

New Relationship Energy (the heightened dopamine and emotional focus that accompanies new intimate connections) presents a specific structural risk in M/s ENM: the slave's cognitive and emotional orientation shifts toward the new person, temporarily weakening the attentional orientation toward the Master that the authority structure requires. Veaux and Rickert (2014) note that NRE causes individuals to "make promises they can't keep" and to underweight existing relationship commitments. In M/s, this is amplified: the slave under NRE may begin to experience the outside connection as a site of relational equality that contrasts with the authority structure, generating cognitive dissonance about the dynamic itself.

Four documented mechanisms address NRE:

  • Check-in intensification. Structured check-in frequency increases during NRE phases. This maintains attentional orientation toward the authority relationship and gives the Master real-time information before emotional momentum consolidates into competing loyalty.
  • Reporting protocols as ongoing practice. The slave reports on interactions with the outside connection continuously, not just at initiation. This is the "inform immediately" protocol operating as a living practice rather than a one-time disclosure.
  • Reorientation rituals. Behavioral anchors (collaring reaffirmation, protocol reinstatement, specific language) that interrupt NRE's tendency to reorganize the slave's relational priorities. Functionally equivalent to Winnicott's holding environment: maintaining the container explicitly during periods when the slave's internal orientation is under centrifugal pressure from outside.
  • Tempo regulation. The Master may regulate the pace of the outside connection's development, allowing the authority structure to absorb the new connection incrementally rather than being required to process a fully formed competing bond after the fact.

There is no peer-reviewed research on NRE management within M/s ENM. Ferrer et al. (2025) developed the Multiple Relationships Maintenance Scale for CNM contexts but did not disaggregate by power-exchange structure. The NRE-specific dynamics within M/s are entirely undocumented at the empirical level. This is a significant gap.

Research Gaps: What the Literature Has Not Yet Answered

  • No empirical study examines M/s relationships within ENM as a distinct structural configuration. Parchev (2023) is the only recent peer-reviewed work treating TPE M/s as analytically distinct from scene-based BDSM, and it does not address ENM intersections. All other research addresses BDSM generally or CNM generally.
  • NRE has not been operationalized in peer-reviewed CNM research. It exists in practitioner discourse (Veaux and Rickert, 2014; Labriola, 2010) but has not been measured empirically. Its specific dynamics within M/s authority structures are entirely undocumented at the empirical level.
  • Compersion within M/s has not been studied. Balzarini (2021) and Synder et al. (2024) do not address whether the emotional dynamics of compersion are structurally modified by authority relationships. The question of whether a Master's compersion differs from a polyamorous primary partner's compersion remains open.
  • No study examines whether M/s structure is protective or destabilizing for primary relationship quality relative to non-structured hierarchical polyamory. Balzarini et al. (2019) establishes quality differentials in hierarchical polyamory generally. The M/s-specific question has not been posed.
  • The TPE contradiction under full ENM is unresolved empirically. Parchev (2023) identifies it theoretically: if all domains fall under the Master's authority, sustained outside connections may eventually generate a structural contradiction between the slave's putative polyamorous agency and the authority structure. Whether this contradiction is resolvable in long-term practice is not documented.
