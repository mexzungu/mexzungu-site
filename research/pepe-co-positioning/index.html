Pepe.co Positioning: Multi-Identity Brand Research

Aug 2026 Bruce, CSO 20 profiles 25 min read

Key Findings

  • Six patterns held across 20 sites. The strongest lead with a single verbal frame, not a resume.
  • Pepe's positioning arbitrage at Scale is pub-authentic legal-adventurer voice. The market is saturated with preacher, academic, and optimizer voices. Only Manson, Noah, and Naval sit in adjacent territory. None is a lawyer.
  • The Now page is a super-weapon. Sivers built it. Everyone respectable stole it. Pepe should ship one week 1 of Iteration 1.
  • Cleanest multi-identity handling at spread scale: Robin Sloan. Tightest voice and product ladder at Scale: Mark Manson. Steal from both.
  • IA depth is a diluting signal. Sites with 4-6 nav items feel like a person. Sites with 14 nav items feel like personal Wikipedia. Iteration 0 lock at 5 items is correct.

1. Executive Summary

Twenty personal sites were analyzed to understand how operators who hold multiple identities (lawyer-plus-founder-plus-writer type patterns) build web homes that grow audience without collapsing into "consultant with a blog." Ten were Spread profiles (small-to-mid audience, multi-domain identity, low commercial pressure). Ten were Scale profiles (Pepe's aspirational tier plus adjacent operators who monetized without losing voice).

Six patterns held across the entire set.

One. The strongest multi-identity sites lead with a single verbal frame, not a resume. Sivers uses life-chapters (musician, entrepreneur, author). Sloan calls himself a "creative industrialist" and lets olive oil, print, and novels sit under that one label. Kleon says "a writer who draws." The frame is the sleeve. The identities are what's inside.

Two. The Now page carries more weight than any other single element in the study. Sivers built it. Everyone respectable stole it. It converts a static site into a heartbeat. Pepe should ship one.

Three. Product ladders differ wildly by scale. Spread profiles typically monetize through consulting, membership, or books-as-authority. Scale profiles run tiered ecosystems (free newsletter to book to paid app to cohort to speaking). Pepe's plan (Misfit Lab pilot to LOF cohort to book to newsletter to podcast) is closer to Scale than Spread. He needs to build the ladder deliberately.

Four. Voice register is where the "pub" reference bites. Almost none of the Scale set genuinely sounds pub-authentic. Sinek is preacher. Ferriss is Silicon Valley-optimizer-guru. Holiday is professorial-Stoic. Only Manson, Noah, and (in odd flashes) Naval hit anything like it. This is Pepe's opening: pub-authentic legal-adventurer is uncontested territory in Scale.

Five. IA depth is a diluting signal. Sites with 14 nav items (Collison, Jocko) feel like personal Wikipedia. Sites with 4-6 nav items (Sivers, Sloan, Manson, Neistat) feel like a person. The current pepecarrillo.co brief converges on 5 items (Who Am I / Now / Writing / What I'm Building / Misfit Lab). Correct.

Six. The best "spread" sites (Sivers, Sloan, Mod, Jaouad, Kleon) treat identity fragmentation as feature. They don't apologize for multi-domain. They flaunt it. The worst compromise sites (Ferriss, Holiday) shove every identity into cramped nav and lose the person under the machine.

What Pepe can steal: Now page, life-chapters framing, editorial project pages, single verbal frame, hand-drawn and analog motifs (from Kleon, Mod, Sloan). What to avoid: Sinek-style preacher polish, Holiday-style logo-parade credentialing, Jocko or Ferriss maximalist nav, Collison's Wikipedia-vibes list.

4. Written Synthesis. Bucket A (Spread)

Identity handling. The Spread cohort's superpower is the single verbal sleeve. Sloan's "creative industrialist" holds novelist plus olive oil maker plus print publisher plus technologist under one label. Kleon's "a writer who draws" holds author plus illustrator. Sivers uses life-chapters as containers: musician (1984-97), entrepreneur (98-08), author (09-25), open-ended future. Mod says "Writer + Photographer" as literal wordmark. Nadia is more diffuse but honest about it. What almost none of them do is list four titles at the top ("lawyer, writer, coach, investor"). That reads as resume. The verbal sleeve reads as person. Direct steal for Pepe: stop leading with the identity list. Find one 3-6 word sleeve that holds lawyer plus techie plus rockstar plus adventurer. Something on the order of "outlaw with a diploma" or "closing deals, chasing horizons." The current tagline "arquitecto de ventures // domador de misfits" gets flagged in Iteration 0 for the wrong reasons but the sleeve instinct is right.

Monetization patterns. Weakest lever: Sivers, Danco, Nadia rely on books plus occasional speaking. Middle: Rao runs consulting plus workshops plus books (Pepe's current shape). Strongest for Pepe's plan: Mod (membership plus books plus shop), Kleon (newsletter plus books plus merch), Colonna (podcast to workshops to coaching to cohorts). Colonna's ladder is the closest architectural match to Misfit Lab plus LOF. Steal: map the Misfit Lab (~500 EUR / mo pilot) as the mid-tier and LOF as the premium tier, matching Reboot's shape (workshops to coaching to bootcamps).

Voice patterns. Sivers, Sloan, Mod, Kleon, Nadia all speak in peer register. No preaching. No optimization guru. No academic distance. The exception is Colonna (mild preacher / Buddhist lean) and Jaouad (literary-meditative). All ten avoid the "let me tell you what I learned" opening. All ten lean into self-deprecation somewhere on the homepage. Steal: peer register is native to Pepe already. Do not drift into preacher when writing about LOF or Misfit Lab. Keep the pub voice.

IA patterns. Range: 3 items (Sloan) to 10 (Sivers, but shallow). Median: 5. Sloan's 3-item nav (Home / About / Moonbound) is remarkable given the diversity of his output. He trusts the reader to explore below the fold. Mod uses 4 top items and stacks newsletters and podcasts in sub-lists. Nadia uses 3. Steal: the Iteration 0 lock (Who Am I / Now / Writing / What I'm Building / Misfit Lab) is at the median. Correct. Don't add a sixth.

5. Written Synthesis. Bucket B (Scale)

Identity handling. At scale, identity fragmentation becomes a nav problem, not a positioning problem. Jocko's 14-item nav and Ferriss's heavy sub-nav both reveal an operator who couldn't decide what to cut. Manson, Neistat, and Noah are the cleanest: 5-6 top items each, sub-identities absorbed under books / podcast / shows. Cal Newport solves it by explicit segmentation: Cal-the-writer, Cal-the-academic, distinct sections with distinct credentials. This works because the two identities share a topic (depth). Pepe's identities share a topic too (misfit plus operator plus adventurer). Segmentation could work if used sparingly. Steal: Newport's segmentation model for a "Who Am I" scroll. Three or four discrete blocks (Lawyer / Writer / Adventurer / Builder) each with credentials and one story, rather than a paragraph blend. Do not do this on the homepage. Do it on /who-am-i.

Monetization patterns. Scale profiles run ladders. Every single one has (a) a free acquisition layer (newsletter or podcast), (b) a mid-tier product (book, course, app), (c) a premium tier (paid membership, speaking, cohort, consulting). Manson's ladder is the tightest and closest architectural match to what Pepe wants: free newsletter to books to paid app subscription to community. Sinek's is bigger but preacher-y. Holiday's is empire-scaled but professorial. Steal: newsletter (Misfit Postcards) as free acquisition, Misfit Lab as $500 / mo mid-tier, LOF as premium annual cohort, book plus speaking as top-of-funnel authority. This is Manson's ladder with Colonna's coaching layer. Blueprint exists.

Voice patterns. Only three profiles in Scale hit anything like pub-authentic: Manson (peer-with-preacher-spikes, closest match), Noah (peer-conversational), and Naval (spare, aphoristic-peer). The rest are preacher (Sinek, Godin, Jocko), academic (Newport, Grant), or Silicon Valley optimizer (Ferriss, Holiday). This is Pepe's arbitrage. The market of Scale personal brands is saturated with preacher voice. A lawyer with a rockstar's ear and a hippie's diary is nearly uncontested at Scale. Steal: Manson's voice register directly. Warm-blunt-funny-slightly-vulgar-serious-when-earned. Studied at the tone profile level, this is Pepe's native voice.

IA patterns. Tightest of the set: Neistat (5 items, all external), but Neistat also owns nothing. Loosest: Jocko (14+). Sinek runs 10 because he's a company, not a person. Manson (6), Noah (5), Grant (6), Newport (7) are all in the healthy band. Steal: stay in the 5-6 band. Do not grow nav as products launch. Push products into sub-pages.

6. Recommendations for pepecarrillo.co

Top 5 tactics to adopt

1. Ship a Now page (Sivers-style)

Highest ROI move on the entire study. Turns static site into heartbeat. Iteration 0 already flags this as P2. Move to P1. Ships week 1 of Iteration 1.

2. Find the single verbal sleeve

Not the identity list. Not the resume. One 3-6 word phrase that holds lawyer plus techie plus rockstar plus adventurer without listing them. Test candidates (feed to positioning workshop): "closing deals, chasing horizons" / "outlaw with a diploma" / "the misfit with a JD" / "lawyer by trade, misfit by nature." This is where Grohl's voice work meets Bruce's positioning.

3. Model the product ladder on Manson + Colonna

Free layer: Misfit Postcards newsletter (Substack, no gate). Mid-tier: Misfit Lab (~500 EUR / mo, ex-direct-reports pilot). Premium: LOF cohort (annual, Q1 2027). Authority top: book plus speaking. Ladder page (later): /work-with-pepe consolidates all four in visual funnel.

4. Editorial project rows, not cards

Confirmed by Iteration 0 lock. Sloan's site does this. Mod does this. Kleon does this. Each project equals full-width row with narrative, not metadata. Steal the exact pattern from robinsloan.com/about (the three-column footer with novels, olive oil, print shop each written as prose).

5. Segmented /who-am-i block model (Newport)

Four scroll sections: Lawyer / Writer / Adventurer / Builder. Each with one line of credentials and one story. Not the current "Four things" grid (which reads Steve Jobs commencement per Iteration 0). Prose, not columns.

Top 3 tactics to avoid

1. Preacher-guru drift

Sinek, Godin, Jocko, Holiday all sit here at Scale. Pepe's positioning arbitrage IS not being them. When writing about Misfit Lab or LOF: peer, not preacher. Pub, not pulpit. Anselmo gate should flag "wisdom-drop" tone.

2. Nav bloat

Ferriss's sub-nav, Jocko's 14 items, Collison's Wikipedia-list are what happens when everything gets a top-level slot. Iteration 0 lock at 5 items is correct. Hold the line.

3. Logo-parade credentialing

Ferriss's investment portfolio row, Holiday's publication logos row, Newport's "MIT-trained" credentialing all read as "please respect me." The GC Powerlist plaque Iteration 0 already flags for demotion falls in this bucket. Marginalia is the right call.

7. Positioning Implications (feed to Stage 3 workshop)

Three positioning axes emerge from the research.

Axis 1. Single-frame vs multi-domain reveal.

Sloan holds four identities under "creative industrialist." Kleon under "a writer who draws." Sivers under life-chapters. Pepe needs to pick which structural pattern he wants. Working hypothesis: hybrid. One 3-6 word verbal sleeve on the hero (candidates above) plus a life-chapters scroll on /who-am-i (Mexico City to NYC to Yale to Camino to Nairobi to Kigali to Barcelona is already the biggest warmth lever Pepe owns, per Iteration 0).

Axis 2. Preacher risk.

Every Scale coaching-adjacent operator (Sinek, Holiday, Grant, Newport, Colonna) drifts preacher. Pepe's coaching cohort (Misfit Lab) is the highest-risk surface. The positioning must actively insulate from this. Working hypothesis: frame Misfit Lab as a pub table, not a pulpit. Copy that reads "come drink with the operators who don't fit" rather than "learn my framework." Distinguish from Sinek's Leaderful and Grant's Rethinking with vernacular voice.

Axis 3. Authority stack.

Ryan Holiday's Stoic plus marketer plus author combo works because the three feed each other (marketer voice explains Stoic, author publishes both). Pepe's stack (lawyer plus techie plus rockstar plus adventurer) is not currently self-reinforcing on the site. Working hypothesis: the lawyer is the load-bearing wall, the rest are the color. All four identities appear. But the frame that sells LOF, Misfit Lab, coaching, speaking, and the podcast is legal-brain-plus-outlaw-life. The rockstar and adventurer are what make him different from every other lawyer. The techie is what makes him different from every other adventurer. The lawyer is what makes him credible to buyers.

Positioning statement (draft, for workshop input):

"A closing-deals lawyer who happens to live like it's still allowed to be a person. Runs Mexzungu Group by day, writes Misfit Postcards by night, teaches lawyers of the future at Lawyers of the Future. Yale-forged, Africa-baked, Barcelona-based. Book coming."

Refine at Stage 3.

9. Findings for Handoff

Top 3 findings

  1. Pepe's positioning arbitrage at Scale is pub-authentic legal-adventurer voice. The market is saturated with preacher (Sinek, Godin, Jocko), academic (Newport, Grant), and optimizer (Ferriss, Holiday). Only Manson, Noah, and Naval sit in adjacent territory. None is a lawyer. None has Pepe's biography. Uncontested lane, high perceived value.
  2. Single verbal sleeve beats identity list, at every scale, universally. Sloan's "creative industrialist," Kleon's "a writer who draws," Sivers's life-chapters, Newport's "computer scientist and bestselling author." All trade an identity list for one label. Pepe's current tagline gets flagged in Iteration 0 for the wrong reason but the sleeve instinct is right. This is the Stage 3 workshop's highest-leverage output.
  3. Product ladder architecture for Pepe equals Manson's shape (newsletter to books to app / community) plus Colonna's coaching layer (workshops to coaching to cohort). Ferriss / Holiday empire model is overkill and off-brand. Sivers / Sloan light-touch model underserves the coaching ambition. Manson plus Colonna hybrid is the target.

Top 2 candidates for Pepe to steal from directly

Robin Sloan (robinsloan.com)

Cleanest multi-identity handling in the Spread set. "Creative industrialist" sleeve, prose-not-cards project descriptions, 3-item nav that trusts the reader, minimal print-editorial aesthetic. This is the exact vibe Iteration 0's "working notebook" metaphor wants to hit.

Mark Manson (markmanson.net)

Tightest voice plus product ladder in the Scale set. "Life advice that doesn't suck" is the tagline template Pepe should benchmark against ("life advice that doesn't suck" to "legal advice that isn't boring" is not the pull; the pull is the tone). Free newsletter to book to paid app is the exact ladder shape Pepe should build.

Feed both to Stage 3 positioning workshop as reference specimens.
