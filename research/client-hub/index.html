Client Hub Design Research

May 2026 Business 7 sources 8 min read

Key Findings

  • The best portals funnel everything into a single view: what is happening, what is waiting for the client, where the money sits. Nothing buried across tabs or separate surfaces.
  • Clients spend less than 30 seconds deciding if a portal is useful. If they cannot locate current status, their outstanding invoice, and their main document in that window, adoption drops to near zero.
  • "Last updated" timestamps are the single cheapest engagement driver. Three characters that convert browsers into users by signaling the portal is live.
  • Firms that communicate proactively retain clients at 50% higher rates. A lightweight email notification on hub update is more valuable than any dashboard feature.
  • Recommendation: Approach A for HavenPay. Static HTML, JSON data file, URL-token auth. Zero new infrastructure. Update in under 5 minutes. Migrate to API-driven when 5+ hubs are active.

1. What the Best Client Portals Get Right

Research across Copilot (now Assembly), Hubflo, ManyRequests, Basecamp client view, HoneyBook, Dubsado, Linear, Moxo, MyCase/Clio, SuiteDash, CoordinateHQ, Softr, and law firm portals (CasePeer, Lawmatics).

One surface, not an inbox plus a dashboard plus a folder

Every tool that succeeds funnels everything into a single view per client. The session starts and the client sees: what is happening, what is waiting for them, where the money sits. Nothing buried. Copilot/Assembly and Hubflo both nail this. Their shared insight: the portal replaces email threads, it does not add another interface on top of them.

Client-facing simplicity vs. internal complexity

The best portals show clients a curated slice of the internal picture. Basecamp's model: everything inside a project is private by default. You explicitly choose what clients see. Clients never see your task backlog, internal comments, resource allocation, or pricing notes.

Visual hierarchy that resolves in 10 seconds

Research is consistent: clients spend less than 30 seconds deciding if the portal is useful. The winning layout: status and progress at top, documents and billing below, messaging or contact at the bottom.

Pending actions surface prominently

The single highest-engagement element across all platforms is the "you have something to do" state. Portals that place this front-center see dramatically higher engagement. For Mexzungu: "awaiting your input" tasks, unsigned engagement letters, unpaid invoices.

"Last updated" is load-bearing

Every portal that tracks client engagement reports the same finding: clients who do not see a recent timestamp assume the portal is stale and stop checking it. An update timestamp on each workstream block is the single cheapest signal of live engagement.

Billing transparency reduces friction

Law firm portals (Clio, MyCase) and accounting portals (ClientHub) converge on the same finding: showing billing status proactively eliminates the most awkward conversation in any client engagement. Clients who can see "Invoice #3, $4,200, due 2026-05-30, status: UNPAID" in their portal pay faster and without prompting.

2. What to Avoid

Feature sprawl

Portals that include messaging, video calls, forms, task assignment, time tracking, resource libraries, and activity feeds fail at all of them. Clients do not adopt tools with more than 3 core functions. For Mexzungu: status, documents, billing. Adding a 4th function before those three are excellent is a mistake.

Internal data leaking into the client view

The most common failure mode in bespoke portals: showing too much. Internal task notes, billing rates, team discussions, draft documents. Clients misinterpret internal state as commitment. Every field visible to a client should pass the test: "what does this communicate if it changes?"

Complexity that requires Pepe to update it

If the update workflow requires opening a CMS, finding the right page, editing a field, and saving, it will not stay current. Portals with the best data freshness are ones where the consultant's existing workflow triggers the update. Friction in the update path equals stale data equals abandoned portal.

Authentication overhead

Portals requiring username and password registration fail with low-frequency clients. Clients who access the portal quarterly will have forgotten their password every time. Magic link authentication is the correct approach for clients at Mexzungu's engagement frequency.

Mobile-hostile design

Research across agency and legal portals shows clients access portals on mobile 60-70% of the time, usually immediately after receiving a notification email. For Mexzungu's Africa-focused clients (HavenPay: Kenya/Uganda), mobile-first is not a preference. It is a requirement.

3. Recommended Feature Set

Must-have (build this first, nothing else matters)

Workstream status blocks

Status badge (Active / Awaiting Client / On Hold / Complete), brief description, last-updated timestamp per workstream.

Tasks in motion with ETAs

Numbered list of active deliverables. Owner (Pepe / Janis / Client) and target completion date.

Document library

Three categories: Engagement Docs, Deliverables, Reference. Name, date added, download link. Google Drive links are fine.

Billing status

Invoice table: number, description, amount, due date, status. Color-coded. No ambiguity.

Awaiting Your Input block

If anything requires client action, it appears at the top in a highlighted callout. Hidden when nothing is pending.

Last-updated footer

Per-page or per-section timestamp. "Hub last updated: 2026-05-10." Non-negotiable.

4. Visual and UX Direction

The mexzungu.com design system already exists and is strong. The client hub should extend it directly. The hub should feel like "you are behind the curtain of something professional and unconventional," not "you are in a SaaS dashboard."

Status badges: three colors, not five

  • Green (Active): Work is in progress, Pepe owns the next step
  • Amber (Awaiting Client): Ball is in the client's court
  • Gray (Paused / Complete): Nothing moving right now

Do not use: "In Review," "Under Development," "Pending Approval," "Blocked." These are internal states. Clients see: Active, Awaiting You, or Done.

Layout: single-page scroll, not tabs

Research shows tabs reduce engagement because clients forget what is behind the other tabs. One continuous scroll from top to bottom: header, awaiting-client callout, workstreams, tasks, documents, billing, footer with booking link.

Mobile first

Card-based layout stacks naturally on mobile. Each workstream card, document row, and invoice row should be full-width on small screens. No horizontal scrolling tables.

5. Architecture Options

Approach A: Fully static with JSON data files (Recommended)

Each client gets a static HTML template at mexzungu-site/clients/[name]/index.html and a JSON data file at mexzungu-site/clients/[name]/data.json. The page reads the JSON at load time via fetch() and renders content client-side.

  • Pros: Zero additional infrastructure. Janis can update via file edits plus deploy.sh. Loads instantly. Version controlled in git. Every change is auditable.
  • Cons: Manual deployment required per update. URL-based auth is not enterprise-grade. Not scalable beyond ~20 clients.
  • Best for: HavenPay pilot, NCA, Duara. Anything up to 10 clients.

Approach B: Cloudflare Pages with API-driven data

Data served from a new route in tools_server.py. Cloudflare Access handles per-client auth with email OTP. Data can be updated without deployment.

  • Best for: 5+ active hubs, when Pepe wants "update once, live immediately."

Approach C: Softr on Airtable or Notion

  • Pros: No-code updates. Softr handles auth and rendering.
  • Cons: SaaS subscription ($49-$149/month). Brand control limited. Data lives outside the brain, creating a sync problem with Janis.
  • Best for: If Kellen needs to update hubs without Janis involvement. Not recommended.

6. Decision Recommendation

Start with Approach A for HavenPay

Zero new infrastructure. Fully within Janis's control. URL-token auth is sufficient for one pre-seed client. Pilot the UX before investing in Approach B.

Migration path: 3+ active hubs: add Cloudflare Access auth, keep JSON architecture. 5+ active hubs: migrate data reads to api.mexzungu.com, enable real-time updates.

First build URL: mexzungu.com/clients/havenpay/?token=[token] | Estimated build time: 3-4 hours
