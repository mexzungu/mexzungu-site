Pepe.co Positioning: Non-US Multilingual Sub-Pass B

Aug 2026 Bruce, CSO 12 profiles 18 min read

Key Findings

  • The US/UK-derived positioning translates cleanly to bilingual markets. Pub-authentic legal-adventurer voice is uncontested in Spanish, African English, and Turkish operator tiers.
  • Bilingual architecture is a solved pattern at the operator scale. Adopt Elif Şafak's model. One domain, header toggle, mirrored URL structure, parallel population.
  • The dual-nationality opener is the non-US warmth lever equivalent to Sivers's life-chapters. Ramos, Şafak, and El-Baghdadi all lead with origin-plus-base-plus-primary-identity.
  • The LatAm coaching lane is a stronger arbitrage than the US market. Either wisdom-guru (saturated) or business-consultant (dry). No operator holds Pepe's exact positioning in Spanish.
  • Watch-out: preacher-guru drift is stronger in LatAm and diaspora coaching markets. Vishen, Iyanla, Deepak Chopra en español, Vusi Thembekwayo all convert commercially but are the exact drift Pepe must guard against.

1. Executive Summary

Twelve non-US operators were analyzed with the same 12 axes as the main research. Nine were verified against live sites. Three were substituted (Fred Swaniker, Facundo Manes, Alejandra Costamagna) after no accessible personal site was found within scope. The set spans Latin America (Jorge Ramos, Andrés Oppenheimer, Rubén Blades, Alejandro Jodorowsky), Spanish-speaking Europe (Amélie Nothomb, Miguel Bosé), Africa (Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie, Aisha Yesufu, Panashe Chigumadzi, Vusi Thembekwayo), MENA (Iyad El-Baghdadi), Turkey (Elif Şafak), the Netherlands via a Portuguese-Brazilian philosopher (Bernardo Kastrup), and Malaysia (Vishen Lakhiani). Baratunde Thurston was included as a US Black operator sitting in the pub-authentic zone Pepe targets. Iyanla Vanzant was included as a diaspora anti-example.

The short answer to the translation question: yes, but with three unlocks and two watch-outs.

Unlock one. The single verbal sleeve pattern holds universally. Ramos leads with "inmigrante y periodista independiente." Nothomb runs single-identity brand ("Fille de diplomate belge, née à Kobé"). El-Baghdadi opens with "Palestinian-Norwegian activist, writer, and public intellectual." Şafak with "Award-winning British-Turkish novelist and storyteller." The dual-nationality opener is the non-US equivalent of Sivers's life-chapters. Pepe's Mexican-Spanish-Barcelona-based identity has the same shape. The main research's "single verbal sleeve" finding is not culturally specific. It is stronger in non-US markets, not weaker.

Unlock two. Bilingual architecture at the operator scale is almost universally a mirrored toggle, not two sites. Şafak's elifshafak.com redirects to elifsafak.com.tr with /home and /anasayfa. Ramos runs ESP/ENG toggle in header with parallel structure. Rubén Blades runs Spanish-primary with English newsletter alternate. Bosé Spanish-only but uses regional platform fragmentation (Mexican, Spanish, Chilean ticket vendors). None of the twelve run separate subdomains or split sites. This is a solved architectural pattern. Pepe's site should be one domain, two languages, mirrored URL structure, toggle in the header. Not two separate builds.

Unlock three. LatAm and African audiences DO accept pub-authentic register. The concern going in was that Spanish-speaking or African audiences might expect more formality than a Manson-style pub voice. They don't. Ramos runs journalist-opinion in vernacular. Blades uses architectural metaphor ("la casa," "la esquina," "el barrio") that reads as street-poet, not academic. Vusi Thembekwayo is aggressive-preacher-optimizer at scale (higher preacher-risk than Pepe should hit but proves the market tolerates a pub register in African English). The only exception is Nothomb, whose French literary register runs cold-formal, but that is the French literary establishment convention, not a general Spanish, African, or Turkish norm.

Watch-out one. Preacher drift is stronger in the "wisdom-guru" tier of LatAm and diaspora coaching markets. Iyanla Vanzant (US Black) and Vishen Lakhiani (Malaysian) both hit full preacher-guru mode with high monetization. Deepak Chopra (referenced but not tabled) sits in the same zone. This tier converts commercially but is exactly what Pepe's positioning must NOT drift toward. The preacher trap is not smaller in non-US markets. It may be larger, especially in Spanish self-help and African diaspora coaching.

Watch-out two. Sparse or neglected personal sites are the norm at the top of the writer/artist tier. Nothomb's site looks like a 2010 publisher microsite. Chimamanda's has a spam injection (Japanese online casino content). Andrés Neuman's site was unfetchable. Alberto Fuguet, Alejandra Costamagna, Roberto Ampuero, Juan Villoro, and Ricardo Arjona all either lacked accessible sites or ran nothing that survived inspection. The Spanish literary establishment does not treat the personal site as a strategic asset. This is Pepe's opportunity, not his obstacle. Building a serious editorial personal site in Spanish plus English is uncontested at his tier. The competitive bar in his exact demographic is much lower than the US-focused main research suggests.

Bottom line. The US/UK-derived positioning translates. Pub-authentic legal-adventurer voice is still uncontested. Single verbal sleeve still beats identity list. Manson plus Colonna product ladder still applies. Add: bilingual mirrored toggle, dual-nationality opener as a warmth lever, and heightened vigilance against the LatAm wisdom-guru preacher trap.

3. Written Synthesis: Bilingual Patterns, Voice Register, Monetization

Bilingual and multilingual site patterns

Six patterns across the set.

Pattern A. Mirrored toggle, one domain, parallel structure

Elif Şafak is best-in-class: elifsafak.com.tr/home and /anasayfa, same tree, header toggle, mirrored nav. Jorge Ramos runs the same shape: ESP/ENG toggle in header, both versions maintain parallel structure. This is the clean architectural pattern.

Pattern B. Primary language + secondary language newsletter alternate

Rubén Blades runs Spanish-primary but offers explicit "English newsletter" signup. Andrés Oppenheimer runs Spanish-primary with English translations of select articles, no toggle. Lighter build than A but signals language flexibility.

Pattern C. Single language, regional platform fragmentation

Miguel Bosé runs Spanish-only but fragments the ticketing layer regionally (eticket.mx in Mexico, El Corte Inglés in Spain, PuntoTicket in Chile). Works for pure commerce sites, not content or brand-carrying sites.

Pattern D. Single language, ignore the other market

Amélie Nothomb runs French-only. Chimamanda runs English-only. Iyad El-Baghdadi runs English-only despite Arab audience. Default for the literary establishment. Not the correct pattern for a bilingual operator brand.

Pattern E. Company-brand as language shell

Vishen Lakhiani uses Mindvalley (multilingual, courses translated) as the main platform and vishen.com as English-primary personal brand. Divide-and-conquer that only works when a company already exists.

Pattern F. Subdomain per language

Not observed in this set. Confirmed by the main research also. Nobody does this at operator scale. Corporate site pattern.

Recommendation for Pepe. Pattern A (Şafak model). One domain (pepecarrillo.co), toggle in header, mirrored URL structure (/who-am-i paired with /es/quien-soy, /now paired with /es/ahora, /writing paired with /es/escritos, /misfit-lab unchanged as a proper noun). Both trees parallel. Newsletter (Misfit Postcards / Postales Misfit) published in whichever language is native to the piece, translated for the mirror. Solved pattern; Şafak proves it scales.

Voice register in Spanish, French, African English, Turkish, Arabic

Spanish-language voice. Ramos runs journalist-opinion vernacular ("Así veo las cosas"). Oppenheimer runs concerned-analyst but not preacher. Blades runs street-poet essayist with architectural metaphor. Jodorowsky runs mystic-artist but reverential, not corporate. There is no evidence Spanish-language audiences expect formality at the operator tier. The formality expectation is a Spanish-language institutional norm (law firms, banks, universities), not a personal-brand norm. Pepe's Spanish tone profile (Mexican slang, "chaparra," "amigo," "hakuna matata," Spanglish code-switching) is native operator register. It will not read as unprofessional in Barcelona or LatAm. It will read as vernacular authenticity, which is uncontested at his tier.

Sub-finding. Castilian second-person plural forms would read as wrong-formal in LatAm. Mexican Spanish forms (ustedes, les, tienen) read correctly in both Barcelona and LatAm. Confirms the standing-rules Mexican Spanish rule as strategically correct, not just personally correct.

French-language voice. Nothomb runs cold-formal literary establishment tone. French literary convention. Not the register Pepe needs. If he ever publishes in French, ignore Nothomb as reference. Deferred.

African English voice. Vusi Thembekwayo runs preacher-optimizer in African English successfully at scale. Aisha Yesufu runs activist-preacher. Panashe Chigumadzi runs professional-literary. Chimamanda runs literary-sophisticated. None sits in the pub-authentic-legal zone. Baratunde Thurston is the closest cross-cultural reference (US Black, pub-authentic peer-activist-comedic). Pepe's African connection makes him distinguishable from every other lawyer, but his voice reference in that market is Baratunde-shaped, not Vusi-shaped. Vusi is what Pepe should not become. Preacher-optimizer converts commercially but is exactly what the main research flagged as the arbitrage-killing drift.

Turkish voice. Şafak runs intellectual-peer with activist undertones. Bilingual and bridge-building. Register model for Pepe when writing about the intersection of multiple identities. Neither preacher nor journalist. The "bridge-builder" voice.

Arabic / MENA voice. El-Baghdadi runs intellectual-activist. Peer, not preacher. English-only reduces sample size but the register is consistent with the peer-not-preacher finding.

Conclusion. Pub-authentic register translates cleanly to Spanish and African English. Şafak's bridge-builder voice is a valuable second reference alongside Manson. Nothomb and Vusi are the two anti-references (French formal and African preacher-optimizer respectively).

Monetization models unique to non-US operators

Pattern G. Subscription-gated intimate community with locational metaphor. Rubén Blades's "El Barrio" is a subscription platform framed as a neighborhood. Ramos's "Lo + Importante" digital channel via Komi.io functions similarly. Both use place-based metaphor (barrio, corner) instead of course/cohort/membership language. Emotionally warmer than the US "membership" convention. Pepe's Misfit Lab could steal this: "The Table," "La Barra," "El Pueblo Chico." Test in Stage 3 workshop.

Pattern H. Publisher-driven direct book sales instead of Amazon. Nothomb links to Albin Michel. Neuman would link to Alfaguara. Chigumadzi to Indigo Press. French / Latin European convention: publisher owns the sales funnel, not Amazon. When Pepe's book ships, do NOT default to Amazon-affiliate as the US operators (Sivers, Kleon) do. Route through the Spanish-language publisher (Planeta, Random House Mondadori, Anagrama) as primary link, with Amazon as fallback. Signals seriousness in Spanish literary market.

Three patterns not worth stealing.

  • Anti-pattern I. Publisher-microsite aesthetic (Nothomb, Chigumadzi). Literary establishment convention that wastes the personal-site opportunity.
  • Anti-pattern J. Nav bloat with domain metaphor (Blades). Steal the metaphor as flavor, not as IA structure. Hold Pepe's nav at 5-6.
  • Anti-pattern K. Spam or abandoned site (Chimamanda's spam injection, Neuman's fetch failure, Fuguet has no personal site). The base rate at the top of the Spanish literary establishment. Pepe's opportunity.

4. Cross-Cultural Specific Findings

Q1. Do Spanish speakers accept pub-authentic register or expect formality?

Yes, at the operator tier. No formality expectation at Pepe's positioning level. Ramos, Oppenheimer, Blades all run vernacular-not-corporate. The Mexican-Spanish register in Pepe's tone profile (Mexican slang, code-switching, warmth) is native operator voice. Reads as authentic in Barcelona, Mexico City, Bogotá, Buenos Aires. Corporate-formal register in Spanish is expected from institutions. NOT expected from personal brands. Do not sanitize the Spanish tone in the mirror site.

Q2. Bilingual operators handling ES + EN as gracefully as Pepe needs to?

Best-in-class: Elif Şafak (TR + EN) and Jorge Ramos (ES + EN). Şafak is the strongest reference. Header toggle, mirrored URL tree, parallel structure, both trees fully populated. Ramos is the strongest ES + EN reference. Blades is the closest reference for a Spanish-primary + English newsletter alternate pattern. Recommendation: Şafak architecture (mirrored toggle) at MVP. Blades architecture as fallback if year-one budget is tight. Never Nothomb/Chimamanda architecture.

Q3. What does the LatAm coaching landscape look like?

Sparse at the operator tier, saturated at the wisdom-guru tier. Spanish self-help / coaching market dominated by Chopra en español, Osho, Louise Hay Spanish, plus Alex Rovira, Jorge Bucay. Full preacher tier. The peer-authentic coaching lane in Spanish is much thinner. Fernando Trías de Bes (Barcelona, ESADE) is one of the few LatAm-relevant peer-register operators. Implication for Misfit Lab. Peer-authentic legal-adventurer coaching is genuinely uncontested in Spanish. Stronger arbitrage than the US market's already-open lane. 500 EUR/mo is above the average LatAm coaching price ($150-300/mo for wisdom-guru tier) but consistent with the operator tier and matches Barcelona buyer expectations. Hold price.

5. Recommendations for Pepe.co Positioning + Bilingual Architecture

On positioning

1. The dual-nationality opener is the non-US warmth lever equivalent to Sivers's life-chapters

Şafak: "British-Turkish novelist and storyteller." Ramos: "inmigrante y periodista independiente." El-Baghdadi: "Palestinian-Norwegian activist, writer, and public intellectual." Pattern: [origin]-[base] + [primary identity]. Pepe's equivalent: "Mexican corporate lawyer turned outlaw operator. Yale-forged, Africa-baked, Barcelona-based." Six words if compressed further: "Mexican-Barcelona-based, outlaw with a diploma." Test in Stage 3 workshop.

2. Add Africa-forged to the sleeve

The main research missed this. In the non-US operator set, biography is more explicit and geographic labels carry more weight. Africa-forged distinguishes Pepe from every European lawyer and from every US operator. Do not bury the Africa layer. Foreground it in the hero, not the life-chapters scroll.

3. The Şafak "bridge-builder" register is a valuable second reference alongside Manson

Manson is the pub voice. Şafak is the bridge-builder voice for when Pepe writes about the intersection of identities (LOF, Misfit Lab, coaching, cross-cultural essays). Neither is preacher. Both are peer. Use Manson for the tactical / newsletter voice; use Şafak for the strategic / positioning-page voice.

4. The LatAm coaching landscape is a stronger arbitrage than the US market

Peer-authentic legal-adventurer coaching in Spanish has essentially no incumbent. Do not sanitize the offer for LatAm. Hold the exact positioning, hold the price, and let the Mexican-Spanish authenticity do the differentiation.

On bilingual architecture

5. Adopt Şafak architecture (Pattern A). One domain, mirrored toggle

pepecarrillo.co/ (English default) and pepecarrillo.co/es/ (Spanish mirror). Header toggle in top-right. Mirrored URL structure. Newsletter (Misfit Postcards / Postales Misfit) published in language native to the piece, translated for the mirror.

6. Fallback: Blades architecture (Pattern B)

If year-one budget is tight, Spanish-primary, English newsletter alternate, English translations of top 20% posts. Upgrade to Şafak-shape when volume justifies.

7. Never Nothomb/Chimamanda architecture (Pattern D)

Single-language, ignore-other-market is the literary establishment default. Leaves half the audience behind. Explicitly wrong for Pepe.

8. Steal Blades's locational metaphor for the community tier

Misfit Lab positioned as "La Barra" (the bar) or "The Table" instead of "membership" or "cohort" reads warmer and is culturally native to Spanish-language audiences. Not a nav change. A framing change on the /misfit-lab page.

9. Route the book (when it ships) through publisher primary, Amazon secondary

In Spanish market, the publisher relationship carries more literary weight. Planeta, Random House Mondadori, or Anagrama as primary link. Amazon as fallback. In English market, Amazon primary is fine.

10. Do NOT copy the two Spanish-language anti-patterns

No publisher-microsite aesthetic (Nothomb). No 14-item nav even with charming metaphor (Blades). Hold 5-6 items even in Spanish mirror.

On voice sanity checks

11. Guard against the Vusi Thembekwayo preacher-optimizer register for Africa-market content

Converts commercially but reads as preacher. When writing about Pepe's Africa work (Duara, Kabisa, Ayembe, Namirembe), the Baratunde peer-activist-comedic register is the correct reference, not Vusi. Anselmo should flag Vusi-style copy as brand-drift.

12. Guard against the Iyanla Vanzant / Vishen Lakhiani wisdom-guru register for Misfit Lab copy

Converts commercially at the wisdom-guru tier but this is exactly what Pepe's positioning is arbitraging AGAINST. Anselmo should flag wisdom-guru copy as brand-drift. Manson and Baratunde are the correct references.
