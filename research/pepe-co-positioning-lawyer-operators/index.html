Pepe.co Positioning: Lawyer-Operators Sub-Pass

Aug 2026 Bruce, CSO 12 lawyer profiles 18 min read

Key Findings

  • The moat holds. Zero of 12 lawyer-operators occupy the pub-authentic legal-adventurer voice register.
  • The lawyer category defaults to three stiff modes: democracy defender, advocacy-populist, or legal-futurist consultant, and none of them read as peer-conversational.
  • Nobody in the lawyer set flaunts simultaneous multi-identity the way Sivers, Sloan, or Kleon do; sequential trajectory or company-brand hiding is the norm.
  • Substack is the category rail (every lawyer with reach in the last 5 years converged there), so shipping Misfit Postcards inside 30 days is the price of entry.
  • The lawyer ladder ceiling is book-plus-speaking-plus-consulting, which leaves a Manson-style paid-app or Mod-style membership tier as genuine blue ocean for the next 12-24 months.

1. Executive Summary

The moat holds. None of the 12 lawyer-operators reviewed occupies the pub-authentic legal-adventurer lane. The closest neighbors are Preet Bharara (calm-authoritative, prosecutor-turned-podcaster) and Joyce Vance (accessible-warm, prosecutor-turned-professor-turned-Substacker with a chicken-and-knitting personal note). Both are democratic-institution defenders speaking to a US politics audience. Neither is a global lawyer-operator. Neither carries adventure, rock and roll, or emerging-markets texture. None sits in the Barcelona / Africa / LatAm / Yale-with-teeth intersection where Pepe lives.

The lawyer-operator category has three dominant voice modes. Democracy defender (Bharara, Vance, Millhiser, Mystal) is the largest and most saturated. Legal futurist and industry critic (Furlong, Henderson, Whelan, Glover, Lat) is the second, and it is uniformly measured, professional, adjacent to management-consulting register. Advocacy-populist (Stoller, Mystal) is the third, angry-with-a-point but rarely warm or adventurous. There is no equivalent of a legal-adventurer who runs a bar in his voice.

One. Every lawyer-operator with real reach in the last five years converged on Substack. Popehat, Original Jurisdiction, Civil Discourse, Stay Tuned, BIG, Jordan Furlong. Substack now functions as the default lawyer-operator distribution layer. Pepe should ship the Misfit Postcards newsletter there (or a durable equivalent) inside 30 days. Missing this rail is missing the category default.

Two. Multi-identity handling among lawyers is almost universally hidden or subordinated. Devin Stone runs three brands (Legal Eagle, Stone Law DC, LegalEagle.tv) but the personal site defers to a single "Passion. Experience. Diligence." legal-boutique frame. Sam Glover reframes as "web developer" after leaving Lawyerist, subordinating his lawyer identity. David Lat ladders "lawyer turned legal journalist, author, and speaker" as sequential trajectory, not simultaneous identities. Bharara centres a rotating expert panel around himself, submerging the person. Nobody in the lawyer set flaunts multi-identity the way Sivers or Sloan or Kleon do. This is not a threat. It is confirmation the identity-sleeve move is a real opening.

Three. Voice register in the lawyer-operator set is stiffer than the general operator set. Even the most accessible (Bharara, Vance, Mystal) do not hit peer-conversational the way Manson or Noah do. This is a real market gap. Pepe's tone-profile study (76 percent messages under 50 chars, "Nop" not "Nope," parenthetical asides everywhere, hakuna matata philosophy) is a voice no lawyer-operator currently owns.

Verdict. Moat holds. Threats within 12 months are low. Position aggressively.

3. Written Synthesis

Multi-identity handling

The lawyer set collapses multi-identity to a trajectory (David Lat: "lawyer turned legal journalist, author, and speaker"), an institutional role (Millhiser: Vox correspondent, lawyer credential hidden), or a company brand (Whelan behind Lawyer Forward; Stone Law DC as boutique register separate from LegalEagle YouTube). Almost none flaunt simultaneous identities the way Sloan flaunts novelist plus olive oil maker or Kleon flaunts writer plus illustrator. The closest lawyer example is Joyce Vance, who lets prosecutor plus professor plus writer plus knitter co-exist on Substack with dry personal notes. This is a rare bright spot in the set, and instructive: her chickens-and-knitting reference is the only pub-authentic signal in the entire lawyer sample. It works. Nobody else is copying it. This confirms the identity-sleeve move (single 3-6 word frame that holds lawyer plus writer plus adventurer plus operator) is uncontested in the lawyer category.

Voice register

The lawyer-operator voice landscape has three modes. Democracy defender: calm, authoritative, professional (Bharara, Vance, Millhiser). Warm but institutional. Advocacy-populist: sharp, moral, angry (Stoller, Mystal). Effective but rarely peer-conversational. Legal futurist and industry critic: measured, high-signal, consultant-adjacent (Furlong, Henderson, Whelan, Lat, Glover). Professional but stiff. None hit pub-authentic peer-conversational. None uses parenthetical asides, hakuna matata register, or the equivalent of "Nop" as a signature verbal tell. This is where the moat lives. Pepe's tone profile (documented at 90k messages, verified across a decade) sits in a voice bucket no lawyer currently occupies. Voice is not a preference. It is defensible territory.

Product ladders

Every lawyer-operator with reach converged on Substack as free-to-paid acquisition, then laddered up through books, speaking, or consulting. Bharara and Vance run the tightest ladders (Substack free to paid to book to speaking to podcast); Furlong runs Substack to consulting; Whelan runs community subs to consulting. Notably absent from the entire set: a Manson-style paid-app-subscription tier, a Mod-style paid membership, or a Colonna-style cohort. The category ceiling stops at book-plus-speaking-plus-consulting, which is the second moat opening. Build a Misfit Lab paid tier and an LOF annual cohort, and there is no lawyer competing at that ladder shape.

IA and single verbal sleeve

Furlong runs a three-value-prop hero ("analyzing plus advancing plus advising"). Stone Law runs the classic three-word "Passion. Experience. Diligence." Lat runs sequential trajectory. Bharara runs a nine-item Substack nav. Nobody in the lawyer set uses a Sivers, Sloan, or Kleon style single verbal sleeve. This is the third moat opening. The lack of a canonical lawyer example proves the sleeve move is genuinely rare in the category, which means adoption will be more distinctive (higher signal, harder to copy fast).

4. Threat and Opportunity Analysis

Anyone occupying Pepe's proposed lane?

No. Zero lawyer-operators in the sample sit in the pub-authentic legal-adventurer voice register with multi-identity flaunted openly. The closest match on tone is Joyce Vance's chickens-and-knitting notes. The closest match on multi-identity trajectory is David Lat (but sequential, not simultaneous). The closest match on ladder shape is Preet Bharara (but democracy-defender voice, not adventurer). No single competitor combines the three.

Threats within 12 months

Low overall. Three modest signals to watch.

Signal 1. David Lat's Original Jurisdiction expands beyond legal-industry gossip

He has the media relationships (NYT, WSJ, WaPo columns) to broaden. If he pivots from insider-industry to founder-adjacent business commentary, he moves closer. Probability: low. Register mismatch persists.

Signal 2. A new lawyer-Substacker adopts Manson-style paid app or community architecture

The category default is book-plus-speaking. Anyone who breaks this and builds a subscription product first would leapfrog. Probability: low in 12 months. High in 24-36 months.

Signal 3. Legal Eagle unifies his split brand

If Devin Stone stops running LegalEagle YouTube and Stone Law DC as separate universes and instead builds a Sivers-style personal home that flaunts YouTube plus firm plus prep, he compresses to a single-sleeve multi-identity lawyer. His audience (3.91M) would give him instant scale. Probability: moderate. But his voice (educator-democratizer) is not pub-authentic. Different lane.

Dormant white space

Lane 1. Voice-first, category-second.

Pepe's opening is register, not topic. Whatever he writes (legal, adventure, misfit-operator memoir), the pub-authentic voice is the moat. Lead with voice, let the identities follow.

Lane 2. Emerging-markets lawyer-operator.

Zero incumbents in the sample write from a Barcelona / Africa / LatAm / Yale-with-teeth intersection. Everyone is US or Canada. This is a positioning wedge nobody is defending.

Lane 3. Simultaneous multi-identity sleeve.

Nobody in the lawyer set flaunts. Everyone hides, sequences, or subordinates. Adopting a Sivers, Sloan, or Kleon style visible multi-identity is genuinely rare, therefore genuinely distinctive.

5. Recommendations for Pepe.co Positioning

Reinforce (from main research)

  • Ship the Now page in week 1 of Iteration 1. Category default in the personal-brand set. Category rarity in the lawyer set. Double advantage.
  • Ship a single 3-6 word verbal sleeve. The lawyer category proves this move has no incumbent. Test candidates: "outlaw with a diploma," "closing deals, chasing horizons," "the misfit with a JD," "lawyer by trade, misfit by nature." Grohl to draft, Bruce to stress-test, Pepe to lock.
  • Keep IA at 5 items. Do not grow.
  • Substack (or a durable equivalent) is category-default distribution. Ship Misfit Postcards within 30 days.

Add (specific to lawyer-operator findings)

1. Flaunt multi-identity, do not sequence it.

Reject the David Lat "lawyer turned legal journalist, author, and speaker" trajectory framing. Adopt the Sloan and Sivers approach: sleeve holds all identities simultaneously. Write the Who Am I page with lawyer plus writer plus adventurer plus operator plus rockstar as co-present, not chronological.

2. Steal Joyce Vance's chickens-and-knitting move at scale.

Weave one non-legal personal texture into every issue of the newsletter. Barcelona, sailing, kitesurfing, family, hakuna matata, Mexican food. This is the single verifiable pub-authentic signal any lawyer in the sample uses. It works. Nobody else does it consistently.

3. Build a Misfit Lab paid tier that no lawyer has.

The category ceiling is book plus speaking plus consulting. A Manson-style app or Mod-style membership at $50-100 per month is genuinely blue ocean in the lawyer set. Design the ladder deliberately.

4. Segment the /who-am-i scroll by identity, not by year.

Four discrete blocks (Lawyer / Writer / Adventurer / Builder) each with one credential and one story. Do not do this on the homepage. Do it on /who-am-i. Copied directly from Cal Newport's cleanest lawyer-adjacent segmentation move.

5. Reject the democracy-defender lane.

It is the largest and most saturated lawyer-Substack lane (Bharara, Vance, Millhiser, Stoller). Pepe's audience is founders, investors, operators, misfits. Never drift into US-politics-institutional register. Reinforce this in Anselmo's brand gate.

6. Reject the legal-futurist consultant lane.

Furlong, Henderson, Whelan, Glover all sit here. The register is measured, professional, stiff. Pepe's voice is the opposite. Never draft in this register.

7. Findings Handoff

Top 3 for Iteration 1

  • Moat holds. Zero lawyer-operators occupy the pub-authentic legal-adventurer voice register. Category default is democracy-defender, advocacy-populist, or legal-futurist. All stiff. All institutional. All easy to differentiate from.
  • Multi-identity flaunt is uncontested. Every lawyer either hides identities, sequences them as trajectory, or subordinates behind a company brand. Adopting a Sivers or Sloan style simultaneous multi-identity sleeve would make Pepe the only lawyer doing it.
  • Substack is the rail, and product ladder ceiling is book-plus-speaking-plus-consulting. Building a paid Misfit Lab tier or LOF cohort at the Manson or Mod ladder shape is blue ocean in the lawyer set. First-mover advantage available for 12-24 months.

Verdict for Iteration 1: Position aggressively. The moat is real, defended by nobody, and cheap to occupy fast.
