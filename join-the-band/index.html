How We Charge Clients
Two components. Both agreed upfront. No surprises.

Mexzungu does not quote hourly rates. Hourly billing turns every client relationship into a negotiation about time. The client watches the clock, the operator watches the meter, and neither is thinking about the outcome. Every Mexzungu engagement is structured around two components: an admin fee that covers the work, and a success fee that rewards the result.

Admin fee
The work, covered.
A fixed fee agreed before the engagement starts. Covers the operator's time, scope, and deliverables regardless of outcome. The client knows what they are paying. The operator knows what they will earn. Paid in stages tied to milestones, not hours logged.
Success fee
The outcome, rewarded.
A percentage of deal value, triggered when the result is achieved. Transaction closes, round is raised, approval is granted. Agreed before work starts, paid on close. Aligns the operator's upside with the client's result. Neither party is guessing what success is worth.
Why this model: the admin fee protects the operator's time if the deal does not close. The success fee ensures the operator is genuinely motivated to get the client to the finish line. The client pays for work done and for results delivered. Both sides know what the other is getting before anyone signs anything. That clarity is part of what they are paying for.
The rule operators follow: never quote hourly, never agree to scope without a number attached, and never discount to win a client. The brand supports premium rates. If a client pushes hard on price, the question is whether they are the right client. Some are not, and that is fine.
The Thing the Agreement Can't Say
The operators who become Principals are not the ones who billed the most.

They are the ones who routed best.

Routing well means this: when you had a client who needed something outside your specialty, you brought it through the network rather than calling a friend outside it. When you could have taken a piece of work and ground through it, you passed it to the person who was genuinely better suited. When a deal you seeded grew into something bigger than you could handle alone, you kept it in the room instead of taking it private.

That behaviour cannot be captured in a percentage point. It is noticed by Pepe. It is visible to everyone else in the network. It is the thing that earns the Principal title, which is the thing that actually matters.

"The networks I've seen break all broke the same way. Everyone optimised for their own cut and stopped thinking about the room. The ones that work are the ones where people route to the best person for the work, every time, even when it costs them something. That's the culture I'm building. If that's not how you think, this probably isn't for you."
Pepe Carrillo, Managing Director, Mexzungu Group
The Rules
What's yours. What's not.

The brand is the moat. It took years to build and it's what makes the economics work for everyone in the network. These rules protect it. They are not negotiable, but they are also not complicated.

What you can do
✓Introduce yourself as a Mexzungu operator in client-facing contexts
✓Use all Mexzungu templates, playbooks, and materials on your engagements
✓List the Mexzungu affiliation on your LinkedIn and professional profiles
✓Co-pitch with Pepe or other operators on qualifying mandates
✓Refer clients into the network and earn your cut on whatever gets closed
✓Set your own rates within the brand's positioning tier
✓Keep your own clients. They are yours when you came in and yours when you leave.
What you can't do
✗Make commitments on behalf of Mexzungu or Pepe without explicit sign-off
✗Use the brand for work outside the agreed scope without approval
✗Take a Mexzungu-originated client direct after you exit. Client ownership rules apply and they are clear.
✗Sub-contract work under the Mexzungu name to third parties without approval
✗Undercut Mexzungu rates in overlapping client relationships
✗Represent Mexzungu in legal or regulatory proceedings without authorization
✗Do something that would embarrass the brand. You know what this means.
