Background

The Situation

What was agreed: From the outset, all staff in the Duara network — School Entrepreneurs, Assistant Teachers, and Educare Fellows — were intended to engage as independent contractors, not employees. This was the agreed model from day one.

What went wrong: Due to a clerical error, a set of employment contracts was circulated to Assistant Teachers instead of the correct consulting agreements. Those contracts were then signed — not by Duara, but by the School Entrepreneurs, who co-signed on Duara's behalf. The SEs do not have signing authority for Duara. As a result, those documents are defective on two counts: wrong contract type, and signed by someone without faculty to bind Duara.

Why it matters: Even defective employment contracts create risk. A court applying the substance test under the Kenya Employment Act could look past the paperwork and find an employment relationship based on how the work is actually performed. The existence of signed employment-labelled documents — even if technically invalid — strengthens that argument. They need to be replaced, and replaced cleanly.

The fix: A structured 5-phase transition to replace all defective contracts with the correct documents, executed properly by Duara. Comms that preserve relationships. A withholding tax regime compliant from day one. And a paper trail — at 30, 90, and 180 days — that reinforces the contractor relationship going forward.

Open Items

OI-01
Withholding tax mechanics: 5% WHT applies to all consulting payments to Kenyan-resident individuals under the Income Tax Act Cap. 470 — SE management fees, AT consulting fees, and EC stipends. Duara deducts and remits directly. No WHT role for the SE. Confirm Duara's KRA remittance process is in place before first payment cycle.
OI-02
Unsigned prior contracts: Approximately 14 ATs may have unsigned employment contracts in circulation. These do not create binding obligations but should be formally cancelled in writing before the new agreements are issued.
OI-03
Dagoretti situation: The Dagoretti SE abandonment involved a safeguarding incident. Any AT at that school needs careful handling — flag to Pepe before contact. Decision memo prepared separately.
OI-04
Departed ATs with potential claims: Any AT who departed without a signed contract and received payment may have a quasi-employment argument. These must be identified in Phase 1 audit and handled with a release letter before the transition is marked closed.
Staff Profiles

School Entrepreneurs

🚀
SE · School Entrepreneur
The Network Owner
Who they are
The SE is the core operator in the Duara model. They identify, open, and run a local school — typically in a community they are embedded in. They are not employees of Duara. They are independent entrepreneurs who license the Duara brand, curriculum, and support infrastructure under a formal agreement.
What they do
Recruit students and families. Hire and manage Assistant Teachers. Run day-to-day school operations. Pay AT fees from school revenue. Report to Duara on enrolment, quality, and finance. Uphold Duara standards and child safeguarding protocols.
Contract Type Signed / In use
Independent Contractor Agreement (ICA)
Duara → SE direct. The SE is a franchisee / operator, not an employee. The ICA covers brand licence, operating standards, revenue sharing, reporting obligations, and termination. Governing law: Kenya.

Why this structure: The SE takes entrepreneurial risk. They are not controlled at a level that creates employment. They can work for multiple principals. The ICA protects both parties — Duara avoids employment liability; the SE gets a clear commercial relationship with defined IP rights.

Teachers

📚
T · Teacher
The Classroom Operator
Who they are
Teachers are qualified practitioners working in Duara partner schools. They hold TSC registration or an equivalent recognised teaching qualification. They are contracted by Duara directly as independent consultants. Not employees of Duara or the School Entrepreneur.
What they do
Deliver the Duara curriculum to learners. Track attendance and progress. Report to the SE on a daily/weekly basis. Attend training and professional development sessions organised by Duara. Submit monthly invoices for payment.
Contract Type v1.0, Under review
Consulting Agreement (Teacher)
Duara to Teacher direct. The Teacher is engaged as an independent consultant by Duara Education. The SE is the day-to-day operational supervisor but is not the contractual party. This keeps Duara in control of the legal relationship and compliance obligations.

Why this structure: Previous employment contracts created statutory obligations (notice periods, redundancy, maternity rights, NSSF/NHIF contributions) that Duara cannot sustainably manage across 30+ schools. The consulting model is appropriate because Teachers typically work part-time, serve specific engagements, and retain the right to work elsewhere.
Structure confirmed, 11 May 2026

Confirmed: Duara contracts the Teacher directly (Duara direct, not SE subcontract). Employment contracts previously in circulation were an administrative error. These are being systematically replaced.

Teachers in Training

🎓
TiT · Teacher in Training
The Developing Practitioner
Who they are
Teachers in Training are practitioners with TSC registration developing their teaching practice through the Duara Teacher Growth Framework. TSC registration is required at the time of signing. They are not employees. They receive a fellowship stipend and structured coaching over a 2-year fellowship period.
What they do
Deliver teaching and learning support under the Duara curriculum. Progress through Stages 1 and 2 of the Duara Teacher Growth Framework over a 2-year fellowship period. Receive coaching from the SE and quality review from Duara. Contribute to school operations as directed. Receive a Duara Teacher Training Certificate on successful completion. TSC-track fellows may progress to a Teacher role (not automatic. SE decision and position availability).
Contract Type v1.1, Ready
Teacher Training Fellowship Agreement
Duara to TiT direct. Structured as a fellowship grant. Not an employment contract and not a consulting agreement. The TiT receives a stipend (not a wage), is enrolled in a defined 2-year programme against the Duara Teacher Growth Framework, and has learning and placement obligations.

Why this structure: The Teacher in Training is genuinely in development. They do not yet hold full teaching credentials, or are actively working toward them. The fellowship structure accurately reflects this learning relationship, avoids employment classification, and creates a clear, positive progression path.
Qualification requirement

TSC registration is required at the time of signing and must remain current throughout the fellowship. If registration lapses or is suspended, Duara may suspend or terminate the fellowship without notice.

Educare

🌱
EC · Educare
The Educare Practitioner
Who they are
Educare practitioners work in the early childhood education layer of Duara partner schools. They are participants in the Duara Educare programme, a structured training and placement programme focused on early childhood development. They are not employees. They receive a programme stipend, structured training, and certification in exchange for their participation and contributions to learners.
What they do
Complete structured training in early childhood education and development. Work in Duara partner schools as part of their practical placement. Contribute to curriculum delivery at the early childhood level. Work toward certification on programme completion.
Contract Type v1.0, Under review
Educare Programme Agreement
Duara to EC direct. The agreement is structured as a programme participation agreement. Not an employment contract and not a consulting agreement. The EC receives a stipend (not a wage), is enrolled in a defined programme, and has learning obligations alongside any operational contributions.

Why this structure: The Educare programme is an investment in the practitioner's development, not a purchase of labour. The programme agreement structure reflects this. Stipend not wage, training obligations, certification outcome. This correctly avoids employment classification.

Comms Guide

  • ✓Frame all outreach as "formalising" the existing relationship — not changing it. The work is the same. The paper is just catching up.
  • ✓Always let the SE brief the AT first before formal contact. The SE is the trusted local relationship.
  • ✓Never use the word "employment" or "employee" in any outreach. Use "partner," "teaching partner," "consulting relationship."
  • ✓If an AT asks "am I an employee?" — the answer is: "No, this is a consulting arrangement, which is standard for the type of work you do with us." Do not elaborate. Flag to Pepe if they push.
  • ✓Email is the primary channel for all HR correspondence — it creates an individual, searchable paper trail that is not shared with other team members. SE briefings can go by WhatsApp. All AT and EC outreach, agreement sends, and touchpoints should use email. If an AT or EC has no email address, set one up for them (Gmail) and teach them to check it — digital access is a basic requirement for working in a tech-enabled network.
  • ✓Any AT who refuses to sign should be flagged to Pepe immediately — do not negotiate individually or offer modifications to the agreement.
  • ✓Departed ATs: do not contact unless specifically instructed by Pepe. The audit identifies them — the decision on whether to reach out sits with Pepe.
  • ✓Signing authority: Victoria signs all agreements on Duara's behalf for now. She may authorize Daphne to co-sign once the process is stable. School Entrepreneurs do NOT have authority to sign on Duara's behalf — this was the original error.
  • ✓Electronic signatures only. Use DocuSign, Adobe Sign, or SignWell (free tier available). Do not use a copy-pasted signature image — it creates audit risk. A proper e-signature creates a timestamped, verifiable record that holds up if challenged.
