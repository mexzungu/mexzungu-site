Gap 01
Deadlock Resolution
—
Does your SHA contain a deadlock provision?
Search the document for the word "deadlock." If you find it, say yes.
Does a concrete resolution mechanism follow it?
A casting vote, a buy-sell clause, or a mediation process counts. "The founders shall discuss" does not.
Gap 02
Leaver Definition
—
Does your SHA use two leaver categories — good leaver and bad leaver?
Search for "good leaver" or "bad leaver." If only "leaver" appears as a single category, that's a no.
Are both categories defined in plain language with different buyout prices?
"As the board may determine" is not a definition. Specific events and specific prices count.
Gap 03
IP Assignment
—
Did any founder create relevant code, designs, or IP before the company was incorporated?
Anything built before the company legally existed — even a prototype, early code, or design files.
Has that pre-existing IP been formally assigned to the company in a signed document?
A separate IP Assignment Agreement, signed by the founder who created it, listing the IP by name.
Gap 04
Drag-Along Threshold
—
Does your SHA have a drag-along clause?
Search for "drag-along" or "drag along."
Is the consent threshold set above simple majority — meaning it could actually force a sale?
If you're 50/50 founders and the threshold is "majority," the clause can only trigger when both of you agree — which defeats the purpose.
Gap 05
Option Pool Expansion
—
Does your SHA address future option pool expansion?
Search for "option pool," "ESOP," or "employee share scheme" — then check if it says what happens when you need to expand it.
Can the pool be expanded without requiring unanimous consent from all shareholders?
If unanimous consent is required, every future pool expansion is a negotiation — exactly when you have the least leverage (trying to close a round).
