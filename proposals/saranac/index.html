Part 1 of 2: Deal Advisory Proposal

What I Heard

You have a deal that is essentially done. The buyer is someone you trust, the valuation is agreed, and both sides want to close without drama. What you do not have yet is the legal architecture that makes the deal real.

You are selling 75% of Saranac Ventures to Woo for $900,000. You retain 25%. Nick stays on as General Manager at $25,000 per year, structured as equity rather than a straight salary so it makes sense for everyone. The business operates in Uganda, which means the land title cannot easily move, so the structure has to work around that, not through it.

What you need is a US entity that holds the economic rights to the Uganda operation, a clean Operating Agreement that governs the 75/25 relationship, a Membership Purchase Agreement that closes the transaction, and the supporting documents that make the land lease and Nick's management role legally defensible. You also need someone to coordinate the US attorney and Uganda counsel so this does not fall apart between jurisdictions.

That is a deal architecture problem. And it is what I do.

Scope of Engagement

Phase 1 — Delaware LLC Formation and Operating Agreement
  • 1 Delaware LLC Formation Guide the formation of Saranac USA LLC via Northwest Registered Agent. Advise on the registered agent relationship, initial filing requirements, and the actions needed to make the LLC the legal and economic owner of the Uganda operation from day one.
  • 2 Initial Operating Agreement (100% Handler) Draft the Operating Agreement for the entity as initially owned 100% by Nick and Lyndsay Handler. Covers governance, decision rights, capital accounts, and the structural baseline before the sale closes.
  • 3 Amended Operating Agreement (75/25 Post-Sale) Draft the amended Operating Agreement reflecting Woo's 75% ownership, covering:
    • Governance structure and board composition (proposed: 2 Woo seats, 1 Handler seat, 1 independent)
    • Profit waterfall: operating costs, reserve fund, staff school fees fund, 75/25 distribution to members
    • Nick's management equity: $25,000 per year as profits interest, guaranteed payment, or capital credit (mechanism confirmed with your US tax advisor)
    • Woo over-contribution mechanism: contributions beyond $225,000 treated as debt at 5%, repaid first at exit
    • Transfer restrictions and succession provisions
Phase 2 — Membership Purchase Agreement
  • 4 Membership Purchase Agreement Draft the agreement under which Woo purchases 75% of Saranac USA LLC for $900,000. Covers representations and warranties, closing conditions, payment mechanics (lump sum or installment, confirmed with the parties), post-closing obligations, and remedies. This is the document that closes the deal.
Phase 3 — Ancillary Documents and Counsel Coordination
  • 5 Land Lease Assignment Agreement Draft the agreement assigning economic and control rights from the Handlers to Saranac USA LLC, while land title remains with the Handlers as required by Ugandan law. Addresses the $1,000 per month rent arrangement and its restructuring within the LLC framework.
  • 6 Management Equity Documentation Document Nick's $25,000 per year arrangement in a form that is defensible with the IRS. The right instrument (profits interest, guaranteed payment, or capital credit) will be confirmed with your US tax advisor before drafting.
  • 7 Counsel Coordination and Closing Coordinate with your US attorney and Uganda counsel through to closing. Prepare strategic briefings, Q&A, and document review checkpoints as needed. Prepare a closing checklist covering all conditions and deliverables.

What We Are Not Doing

We are not providing US legal advice or appearing as counsel of record. We are not advising on Ugandan land law or the conservation zone implications specific to your lease. We are not providing US tax advice on the capital gains treatment of the $900,000 sale or the mechanics of Nick's management equity. Those require a US attorney and a US CPA, and we will help you find them if you do not already have them.

Part 2 of 2: Engagement Letter

Why does this letter exist?

Because you are about to close the biggest deal of your ecolodge journey, and the paperwork needs to keep up with the ambition. You are selling 75% of the business to a trusted buyer while keeping skin in the game. There is a Uganda land title that cannot be moved, a Delaware LLC to be formed, an operating agreement to write, and a purchase agreement to close. This letter captures what we are doing together, what it costs, and how we treat each other along the way. Sign it and let's move.

Where does the Studio sit, geographically and legally?

The Studio is a Mauritius company. We work across borders: Uganda, Delaware, wherever the deal lives. We are not licensed attorneys in the United States, Uganda, or anywhere else. We are deal architects: we structure the transaction, draft the documents, manage the process, and coordinate specialist counsel on both sides. You will need a US attorney to review and execute the Delaware filings, and Ugandan counsel for anything touching the land. We will help you find both if needed.

1. What are we working on together?

You are selling 75% of a US entity that will hold the economic rights to Saranac Ventures and Ecolodges Ltd, a Uganda ecolodge business, to a buyer you know ("Woo"). The Handlers retain 25%. Nick stays on as General Manager at $25,000/year, structured as equity rather than cash compensation. The Uganda land title stays in the Handlers' names by legal necessity, with economic and control rights assigned to the US entity by agreement.

The deal has three moving parts and we are covering all three:

Phase 1: Delaware LLC Formation and Structure. Guide the formation of Saranac USA LLC via Northwest Registered Agent ($50 formation + $90 state fee + $125/year registered agent). Draft the Operating Agreement for the initial 100% Handler-owned entity, then the amended OA reflecting the 75/25 post-sale structure. Cover governance (board composition: 2 Woo seats, 1 Handler seat), profit waterfall (ops costs, reserve fund, staff school fees fund, then 75/25 to members), and Woo's over-contribution mechanism (amounts beyond $225k treated as debt at 5%, repaid first at exit).
Phase 2: Membership Purchase Agreement. Draft the agreement under which Woo purchases 75% of Saranac USA LLC for $900,000. Cover representations and warranties, closing conditions, payment mechanics (lump sum or installment, to be confirmed with the parties), and post-closing obligations on both sides.
Phase 3: Ancillary Documents and Coordination. Draft the management equity arrangement for Nick (converting $25k/yr to profits interest, guaranteed payment, or capital credit, the right mechanism to be confirmed with US tax counsel). Coordinate all documents with your US attorney and Ugandan counsel for execution. Prepare Q&A and strategic briefings on any open structural questions as they arise.

2. What do you actually walk away with?

(a) A clean Delaware LLC with an operating agreement that governs the 75/25 structure, the profit waterfall, and Woo's economic rights.
(b) A membership purchase agreement that protects the Handlers on representations, warranties, and payment.
(c) A land lease assignment that gives Woo real economic control without triggering Uganda's foreign ownership restrictions on the title.
(d) A management equity arrangement for Nick that is documented and defensible with the IRS.
(e) A closing checklist and process that gets this across the line without surprises.

3. What are we assuming going in?

(a) On the buyer: Woo is a friendly counterparty who has accepted the $1.2M implied valuation and is comfortable with the US-entity-only structure (no immediate Uganda title transfer). If the buyer's position shifts materially, we talk and adjust scope.
(b) On US tax: Nick's management fee-to-equity conversion and the capital gains treatment on the $900,000 sale require a US-licensed CPA or tax attorney. We will flag the right questions but we are not providing US tax advice. If you do not have a US CPA, we will help you find one.
(c) On Uganda land: The land title stays in the Handlers' names. Ugandan counsel should confirm there are no issues with the land lease assignment structure given the conservation zone location. This is outside our scope to advise on directly.
(d) On timeline: We are treating this as an active deal with a target to close within 60-90 days. If the timeline shifts, the monthly retainer keeps running until close or termination.
(e) Documents from you: When we need anything (title documents, the existing Uganda company structure, Woo's entity details, financial records), we need them within 5 business days. Late documents shift the timeline accordingly.

4. What are we not doing?

(a) Filing anything with any court, registry, notary, or government office in any jurisdiction.
(b) Providing US legal advice or appearing on your behalf in any US legal proceeding.
(c) Providing Ugandan legal advice, including on foreign ownership rules, conservation zone compliance, or land title transfer.
(d) Providing US tax advice on the capital gains treatment of the $900,000 sale or the mechanics of Nick's management equity.
(e) Replacing your US attorney or Ugandan counsel. We coordinate with them; we do not substitute for them.
(f) Anything not described in Section 1.

No. This is deal architecture and document coordination. We give you the structure, the drafts, and the strategic logic. We do not file papers or sign legal opinions. Think of us as the deal partner who has closed enough transactions across enough jurisdictions to know which questions to ask and how to build documents that hold up when the lawyers review them.
