Deal Advisory Proposal

What we heard

You have a deal that is essentially done. The buyer is someone you trust, the valuation is agreed at $900,000 for 75%, and both sides want to close without drama. What you do not have is the legal architecture that makes any of it enforceable.

You need a US entity that holds the economic rights to the Uganda operation, a clean Operating Agreement that governs the 75/25 relationship, a Membership Purchase Agreement that closes the transaction, and documentation that makes the land lease and Nick's management role defensible after close. You also need someone who has done this exact structure before to coordinate your tax advisor and Uganda counsel.

What breaks if you don't act

Three things put this deal at risk before it closes.

1
There is nothing to sell. Woo cannot legally acquire a percentage of an unincorporated Uganda business. Until Saranac USA LLC exists and holds the economic rights to the operation, there is no equity to transfer. Every week without the entity is a week the buyer waits, and buyers who wait find reasons to renegotiate.
2
Woo's $900,000 is buying a handshake. Without a formally documented assignment of the land lease's economic and control rights to the US entity, the most valuable asset in the deal has no defensible chain of title. If Woo brings a lawyer into the conversation and that lawyer asks "what exactly does the LLC own," the answer right now is: nothing documented. That is a negotiating lever against you at close.
3
Nick's position after close is unprotected. The $25,000 per year "as equity" has no legal form until it is documented as a profits interest, guaranteed payment, or capital credit in the Operating Agreement. Without it, it can be disputed, modified, or ignored by the 75% majority owner the day after signing.

What we build

Phase 1 — Delaware LLC and Operating Agreement
  • 1 Delaware LLC Formation Guide the formation of Saranac USA LLC through a registered agent. Covers the registered agent relationship, initial filing requirements, and the steps to make the LLC the economic owner of the Uganda operation from day one.
  • 2 Initial Operating Agreement (100% Handler) Draft the OA for the entity as initially owned 100% by Nick and Lyndsay. Covers governance, decision rights, capital accounts, and the structural baseline before the sale closes.
  • 3 Amended Operating Agreement (75/25 Post-Sale) Draft the amended OA reflecting Woo's 75% ownership: governance structure (2 Woo seats, 1 Handler seat, 1 independent), profit waterfall (ops costs, reserve fund, staff school fees fund, 75/25 distribution), Nick's management equity, and Woo's over-contribution mechanism (amounts beyond $225k treated as debt at 5%, repaid first at exit).
Phase 2 — Membership Purchase Agreement
  • 4 Membership Purchase Agreement Draft the agreement under which Woo purchases 75% of Saranac USA LLC for $900,000. Covers representations and warranties, closing conditions, payment mechanics, post-closing obligations, and remedies. This is the document that closes the deal.
Phase 3 — Ancillary Documents and Coordination
  • 5 Land Lease Assignment Agreement Draft the agreement assigning economic and control rights from the Handlers to Saranac USA LLC, while land title remains with the Handlers as required by Ugandan law.
  • 6 Management Equity Documentation Document Nick's $25,000 per year arrangement in a form defensible with the IRS. The right instrument (profits interest, guaranteed payment, or capital credit) confirmed with your tax advisor before drafting.
  • 7 Coordination and Closing Coordinate with your tax advisor and Uganda counsel through to closing. Prepare strategic briefings, Q&A, and a closing checklist covering all conditions and deliverables.
