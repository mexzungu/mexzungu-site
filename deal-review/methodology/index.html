Deal Review Framework

How we score an investment deal

Every deal review we produce uses the same 5-dimension rubric. 100 points total. No guesswork, no vibes. Here is exactly how we get to the number.

The score answers one specific question: if you signed the final documents today, exactly as written, what would your risk be? It is not a verdict on the underlying business. It is a snapshot of what you are agreeing to in the current draft. Most deals improve significantly with negotiation.

The Framework

We evaluate every deal across five dimensions, each worth 20 points. The dimensions cover structural integrity, investor protections, financial transparency, exit potential, and process conduct. A perfect score is 100. The risk label tells you what to do with the number.

1
Structure & Legitimacy
20 pts

Is the legal entity clean, properly formed, and operating in a jurisdiction you understand? Can you verify what you are actually buying?

Entity type
Is the structure (LLC, Corp, SPV) appropriate and verifiable? Do we know the governing law?
Equity class
What are you getting exactly? Voting rights, economic rights, priority on liquidation?
Cap table
Who else owns the company? Are there senior classes ahead of you? Debt that ranks above equity?
Valuation basis
How was the valuation set? Is there an independent appraisal or is it a number someone picked?
2
Investor Protections
20 pts

What mechanisms exist to protect your investment after the wire goes out? Unprotected minority equity is very close to a gift.

Information rights
Will you receive periodic financial reports? Annual statements? Can you audit?
Anti-dilution
If they raise more capital at a lower valuation, does your stake get protected?
Tag-along rights
If founders sell, can you sell alongside them on the same terms?
ROFR / pre-emption
First right to buy new shares or existing shares before outsiders? Prevents dilution and protects exit.
3
Financial Transparency
20 pts

Can you verify the financial claims being made? Does the company have clean books, audited statements, and a credible use-of-proceeds plan?

Financial health
Audited or reviewed financials available? What does revenue, margin, and debt look like?
Valuation methodology
DCF, comps, asset-based? Is the implied multiple defensible relative to the sector?
Use of proceeds
Where is the capital going? Is there a budget? What changes after you invest?
Tax pass-through
For US LLCs: will you receive a K-1? What are the tax implications in your jurisdiction?
4
Exit & Return Path
20 pts

How do you get your money back? An investment with no exit mechanism is closer to a loan you will never see repaid.

Exit mechanism
Is there a defined path? Sale, buyback, IPO, or redemption clause? Timeline attached?
Return expectations
What is the projected multiple? Is there a distribution policy for cash-flowing businesses?
Market opportunity
Is the sector growing? Is there a realistic path to growth that justifies the entry price?
Dissolution priority
If the business closes, what priority do you have on remaining assets? Ahead of or behind whom?
5
Process & Conduct
20 pts

How is the deal being run? Pressure tactics and rushed timelines are not coincidences. They are signals about how the relationship will operate post-close.

Documents before wire
Are final, signed documents delivered before any money moves? Non-negotiable.
Time pressure
Are you being pushed to decide in days? Artificial urgency is a red flag in any asset class.
Independent review
Has the issuer encouraged or allowed independent legal and financial review?
Track record
Can you verify the management team's history? References? Prior investors you can call?

Risk Score Thresholds

80+
Low Risk
Well-structured deal with strong protections. Proceed with normal diligence.
60-79
Moderate Risk
Acceptable with negotiated improvements. Identify weak dimensions and push back.
40-59
High Risk
Significant gaps. Only proceed if terms can be meaningfully improved before close.
20-39
Very High Risk
Multiple structural failures. Proceed only if you can afford to lose the full amount.
<20
Do Not Proceed
Fundamental failures across multiple dimensions. Walk away.

Our Absolute Minimums

■

Information rights required. If you cannot get periodic financial reports in writing, you cannot monitor your investment. This is non-negotiable regardless of deal size.

■

Docs before wire, always. No final documents, no money. Any deal where funds are required before paperwork is signed has a structural integrity problem.

■

Independent review over $25k. For investments above $25k, independent legal review is the floor. Not optional.

■

At least one exit mechanism. A buyback clause, a sale provision, a redemption right. Something. An investment with zero exit path is not an investment.
