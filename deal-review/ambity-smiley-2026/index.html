Dimension-by-Dimension Scores
Dim 1
Structure & Legitimacy
3 / 20
The LLC is a Texas entity acquiring a dental practice, which is a legitimate structure. However: Class B shares are explicitly non-voting per Sections 2 and 3 of the term sheet. There is no independent valuation methodology disclosed. The $12.5M post-money valuation for a single dental practice acquisition has no supporting documentation in the term sheet. The governance structure gives investors zero voice in how the company is run.
Dim 2
Investor Protections
5 / 20
No information rights disclosed in the term sheet. No anti-dilution protection. No tag-along rights. No ROFR or pre-emption rights. No mention of investor board representation. The document is silent on every standard minority protection mechanism. An investor putting in $100k receives zero contractual visibility into what happens to that money afterward.
Dim 3
Financial Transparency
2 / 20
No financial statements referenced. No audited accounts for Smiley Dental and Orthodontics. No revenue or EBITDA figures disclosed. No clear use of proceeds beyond the general acquisition purpose. The valuation is stated as fact without any supporting financial model or comparable transactions. For a $12.5M implied valuation on a single dental practice, the absence of any financial data is a significant gap.
Dim 4
Exit & Return Path
10 / 20
Dental practice ownership is a legitimate and relatively stable business model with known cash flow characteristics. The DSO (Dental Service Organization) sector has active M&A markets, which provides a theoretical exit path via a future sale. However, the term sheet contains no explicit exit mechanism, no buyback provision, no redemption clause, and no timeline. The sector opportunity is real; the contractual path to monetize it is absent.
Dim 5
Process & Conduct
6 / 20
The August 21 closing deadline creates real time pressure on a $100k investment decision. The term sheet requests wire transfer before final documents are confirmed to be in investor hands. No mention of independent legal review being encouraged or provided for. No track record or management biography included in the materials reviewed. These are process red flags independent of whether the underlying business is legitimate.
Red Flags
Wire before documents. The term sheet structure implies funds transfer ahead of final signed documentation. This is the single most reliable indicator of a poorly structured deal. No exceptions.
No information rights. Once your $100k is in, you have no contractual right to any financial update unless it is added to the final documents. You would be flying blind.
No exit mechanism. There is no buyback, no redemption right, no put option. Your equity could be worth exactly what it is today indefinitely, with no path to liquidity.
No anti-dilution protection. Future rounds could dilute your 0.80% to near zero with no recourse.
Unexplained valuation. $12.5M post-money for a single dental practice acquisition with no financial statements, no comparables, and no independent appraisal disclosed.
Artificial deadline. August 21 closing on a $100k investment decision with incomplete documentation reviewed. Pressure tactics are a negotiation strategy, not a reason to rush.
Class B = non-voting in this deal. Defined explicitly in Sections 2 and 3. This is not universal for Class B shares, but it is what this company chose. It is negotiable before signing.
Questions to Ask Before Signing
On structureWhere exactly is our 0.80% reflected in the cap table? Who else holds equity and at what class?
On the valuationHow did you arrive at a $12.5M valuation? Can we see the financial model or the independent appraisal?
On information rightsWhat financial reporting will investors receive? Quarterly statements? Annual audit? Who prepares them?
On exitHow do investors get their money back? Is there a buyback option, a distribution policy, or a target sale timeline?
On processWill investors receive final signed documents before any wire is sent? Can we have 30 days for independent legal review?
On anti-dilutionIf the company raises future capital at a lower valuation, what happens to our percentage?
On Class BThe term sheet defines Class B as non-voting. Is there any scenario where Class B holders get a vote? Is this term negotiable?

Bottom Line

This deal scores 26/100 on our risk framework. That puts it in the Very High Risk band. The underlying business, dental practice ownership in the U.S., is a legitimate sector with real cash flow potential. The problem is not what they are buying. The problem is how they are asking you to invest in it.

No investor protections. No financial transparency. Wire before documents. An August 21 deadline. These are not details to negotiate around the edges. They are the deal structure. Before putting $100k into this, every one of the red flags above needs a written answer that goes into the final investment agreement.

If they push back on adding information rights, anti-dilution, or documents-before-wire as requirements, that answer tells you everything you need to know.
